# M1: the companion

**Status:** built, waiting on its smoke test on the owner's deployment (VISION rule 7). Started 2026-10-21. [HANDOFF-M1.md](HANDOFF-M1.md) has where M0 left things.

M1 proves that Atlas feels like Atlas in conversation and knows your stuff (VISION §25). It adds:

- **the full loop:** budgets (done in M0), compaction (§4.1) and basic helpers (§8.1);
- **the log, records and recall:** records written during the turn with a chip (§5.4), and the `recall` tool (§5.3);
- **areas** (§6): tagging with the `decide` role, the scope switcher, and per-area settings;
- **the character** (§23.1): the Character page with presets, and old Atlas's character eval;
- **the UI v1 shell** (§21, [docs/ui/](ui/README.md)): Home with the stage and console, Inbox, Agenda, Automations, Library, History and settings, with the first views, receipts, and the Graphite and Daylight themes;
- **setup steps 1 to 5** (§22), with passkeys replacing the owner secret.

**Exit:** the smoke test (§1) passes on the owner's deployment, and M0's smoke test still passes ([M0.md §4](M0.md#4-the-smoke-test-and-what-it-found)).

## 1. The smoke test

Written before the build (VISION rule 7). Run it on the owner's deployment after every deploy that touches the core, then M0's list. Each line should just work. When one doesn't, the fix goes in the character, the context, a tool's description or the tool (rule 1), and the finding goes in §4.

**Setup** (§22)
1. A fresh deploy opens on setup. Enter the setup code (the owner secret chosen at deploy) once and create a passkey. Sign out, and sign back in with the passkey alone.
2. Pick a name, a voice (each one heard before choosing) and a personality preset.
3. Pick a brain: sign in with ChatGPT, or keep Workers AI. Each brain is tested live, with its speed shown.
4. Connect iCloud Calendar with an app-specific password. Setup says what it found ("3 calendars and 214 events in the next 30 days").
5. Atlas looks: it proposes areas (each one tap to accept, rename or merge), key people, and three to five things worth knowing about the week.
6. From the deploy button to step 5 takes under 10 minutes, with nothing in a terminal after deploy. Every step can be skipped and picked up again from settings.

**Memory** (§5.4)
7. "I'm vegetarian." A chip shows it was noted, with Undo.
8. Later, in a fresh run: "Pick somewhere for dinner Friday." The answer fits without reciting it ("since you're vegetarian…" is a fail).
9. "Actually I eat fish now." The record is corrected, not duplicated; Library shows the old value in its history.
10. "Forget that I'm vegetarian." It's gone from Library and from what Atlas uses. History shows both the save and the forget.
11. Undo on a "Noted" chip removes the record; Undo on a "Forgot" chip brings it back.

**Recall** (§5.3)
12. "What did I say about the Riverside shoot last week?" finds it, quotes or summarizes it, and says when and where it was said.
13. A question that needs meaning, not the same words: "what was the thing with the camera rental?" when the thread said "lens hire".
14. A question by time: "what did we talk about on Tuesday?"
15. A question by links: "anything with Jordan about Riverside?"
16. By voice, step 12 gives an honest status line, then the same answer as text.

**Compaction** (§4.1)
17. A conversation long enough to pass 70% of the context window keeps going. A commitment made at its start ("remind me Friday to send the invoice", or a fact said once) still holds afterwards.
18. History shows the save-what-matters pass and the summary as their own rows.

**Areas** (§6)
19. After a few conversations about work and home, Library → Areas lists them, each with what's in it.
20. A new message about work gets the work chip. Moving the chip to another area re-tags the related records, and later messages like it follow the correction.
21. The scope switcher filters every page to one area. Atlas still answers questions about any area.
22. An area's tone setting changes how Atlas answers in it.

**Character** (§23.1)
23. The Character page shows the presets, the four scales and a box for your own words, with a sample reply that changes as you move them. Saving changes the next reply's manner, and Atlas is the same mind with the same memory.
24. Check character (in Settings, or `npm run eval:character`) passes all sixteen prompts for every brain, each judged by a different brain: old Atlas's bar.

**Helpers** (§8.1)
25. "Go through everything we've said about Riverside and list the decisions." Atlas hands it to a helper; the jobs button shows it running with what it's doing, the answer comes back in the same reply, and History shows the helper's lookups.
26. "Check the next month for clashes, no rush." Atlas says it'll report back; the job runs in the background, and its result arrives in the thread when it's done.
27. Start a background job, then redeploy while it runs. After the restart it carries on from where it was and still reports back. Stop on another running job ends it, and Jobs shows it stopped.

**The UI** (§21)
28. Home: the stage (Now and Week) and the console with the last exchange and receipts. ⌘J opens the full transcript.
29. A receipt opens into its view on the stage.
30. Inbox, Agenda, Automations, Library and History each open and show real data (empty states where there's none yet).
31. Every page works in Graphite and Daylight, at desktop and phone width, and with reduced motion.

**Failure paths**
32. Turn the embedding model off (or break it). Recall still finds things by keyword and time, and says meaning search is down.
33. Refresh mid-answer. The saved answer appears once it's done.
34. A passkey from another device is refused, with a plain reason.

**Regression**
35. M0's smoke test passes.

## 3. Deploy it

M1 deploys the same way as M0 ([M0.md §1](M0.md#1-deploy-it)): `npm run deploy`. The owner secret you set for M0 is now the **setup code**.

What happens on the first start after deploying over M0:
- The database migrates on its own (a `state` table, memory, areas). Nothing you saved is lost.
- In the background, Atlas indexes M0's whole thread for recall, embeds it, tags it with areas, and, if the thread is long, summarizes the older part a chunk at a time. Replies don't wait for any of it. The Worker log has a line for anything that fails (`memory.embed`, `areas`, `compaction`).
- You sign in again: M0's sign-ins don't carry over, because sessions are now kept by Atlas so they can be ended. Use your setup code, and make a passkey when it offers. Setup opens once, so you can add a passkey and skip the rest; it's in Settings → Your data if you want it again.
- The setup code must be at least 16 characters. If the secret you chose for M0 is shorter, the sign-in page says so; set a longer one with `npx wrangler secret put ATLAS_OWNER_SECRET` and reload.
- Look over Settings once. Since M0, changing one setting by itself (the time zone on first connect, or the brains after ChatGPT sign-in) could put the others back to their defaults. M1 fixes that, but anything already reset stays as it is: Atlas's name, your name, the brain chains and the voice.

**Things to check on your account.** These couldn't be checked from the build sandbox, which has no Cloudflare account. Each is isolated in one place.

| What | Where | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| EmbeddingGemma | `core/src/brains/embed.ts` | Recall by meaning works (smoke step 13). If it doesn't, the Worker log's `memory.embed` lines and recall's own note say what Workers AI answered. |

## 5. Decisions made while building

- **Non-secrets moved out of the secrets table.** The iCloud calendar list, the note that iCloud refused the password, and the ChatGPT client's details (host id, email, models) now live in a small `state` table (`core/src/store/state.ts`), moved by a migration. The ChatGPT client keeps its last ID token there too, since it's only a hint to OpenAI's sign-in page, not something that grants access; the scrubber still keeps it from leaving.
- **`agent.ts` is split by job.** `core/src/agent/` holds the test seams, the calendar sense with its refusal guard, ChatGPT sign-in and refresh, and the settings view with its live tests. `agent.ts` keeps the socket, the runs and the clock.
- **Recall searches inside the agent's own SQLite, not a separate vector index.** VISION §19.1 named a vector index. Each person's memory lives in their own agent, so its index lives there too: SQLite's full-text search (FTS5, with stemming) for keywords, and embeddings stored beside each item and compared in the agent. That keeps one store per person, works in tests, and needs nothing created at deploy. Meaning search compares the most recent 5,000 items when no time range is given, and every item in range when one is. If one person's log outgrows that, a Vectorize index can take the meaning half without changing the tool. VISION §19.1 is updated to match.
- **Embeddings are Workers AI's EmbeddingGemma** (`@cf/google/embeddinggemma-300m`: 768 numbers per item, multilingual). It comes with the account, so recall works on a fresh deploy. Each stored embedding names its model, so a later change of model re-embeds instead of mixing two kinds. The model is a constant for now; it becomes a choice on the Brains page with the rest of the roles.
- **Memory never slows a reply.** New messages are indexed and embedded after each run ends, in the background, and on start (which also indexes M0's whole thread once, in batches). `recall` adds any messages not yet indexed, and embeds stragglers beside the query (in a separate call, so no stored text can hold the query up), so it never misses the last few minutes. An embedding is only stored if the text didn't change while it was being made.
- **One text the model refuses can't stall meaning search.** When a batch fails, the background pass tries its texts one at a time; if any go through, the ones that don't are marked as refused and skipped from then on. If none go through, the model is down: nothing is marked, and the reason goes to the Worker log. Texts are cut to 2,000 characters, inside EmbeddingGemma's input.
- **Meaning matches have a floor.** A match must be at least 0.3 similar, and within 0.2 of the best match, so a question with no real match gets nothing instead of the least-bad items. Both numbers are guesses until they're checked against real EmbeddingGemma scores on the deployment (smoke test step 13).
- **Results are merged by rank.** Keyword, meaning, time and link results are combined by reciprocal rank, so an item near the top of any list, or in several, comes first. Records that recall keeps returning get a lift worth less than one place, which breaks near-ties without burying a better match (§5.3).
- **"Forget" leaves the log alone.** A forgotten record stops being used and leaves recall, and so do the message it was learned from and every message that corrected it, so recall can't bring it back. A correction keeps where the record was first learned as its source and adds its own message beside it. The log keeps the messages and every record change (§4.2), and Undo on either chip reverses it. Undoing a correction is refused if the record changed or was forgotten since, so a later change is never silently lost.
- **Saying the same thing twice corrects instead of duplicating.** `remember` without an id updates an active record of the same type and name (any case), keeping the old value in history.
- **Standing facts are preferences** (and the owner's own record, once setup creates it). The newest 100 sit at the end of the stable block, oldest first, with ids so Atlas can correct one. Everything else is found with `recall`.
- **The clock's runs can't save facts about the owner** (§5.4: never from Atlas's own work). Runs Atlas starts itself get `recall` but not `remember` or `forget`.
- **A run sees the summary and everything since it**, instead of M0's last 40 messages. The summary sits at the end of the stable block, since it only changes when compaction runs, so it caches with the character and the standing facts.
- **Compaction plans for a 128k-token window.** Providers don't report their windows, and every brain Atlas offers takes at least that. It starts at 70% (counting four characters to a token), keeps the newest quarter of the window and at least six messages, and never cuts between a question and its answer. One pass takes at most 40% of the window, oldest first, so its own prompt always fits; a context far behind (the owner's M0 thread, on first start) catches up over several passes. If it falls behind, a run leaves out the oldest messages to stay under 85% of the window; recall still has them.
- **Voice reads less history.** A voice turn reads at most about 12,000 tokens of the conversation, roughly M0's last 40 messages, because a long prompt slows a model's first words. It has the same summary, facts and recall as text, so it's the same mind with a shorter view of the last hour.
- **Compaction runs in the background after a run ends**, never in the way of a reply. The save pass gets `remember` and `recall` (the owner's own words, so §5.4 allows it, and the records say they came from compaction); the summary gets no tools. After a failure it waits ten minutes before trying again, remembered across restarts, and the reason goes to the Worker log and the failed run. Its failures stay out of the thread: they're Atlas's upkeep, not something the owner asked for.
- **Helpers are Agents SDK sub-agents running Atlas's loop inside a durable fiber** (VISION §19.3, decided 2026-10-22). A helper saves a checkpoint after each step and, after a restart, carries on from it. It holds nothing of the owner's: its brain calls, tool calls and log rows all go through the parent, so there is still one router, one set of sign-ins, and one log. Its own storage only holds its checkpoint.
- **Helpers can only look** (`recall`, `calendar_read`, `reminder_list`) and can't start helpers. That keeps §5.4 (no facts from Atlas's own work) and §8.1 (never more powers than the parent), and it makes a step re-run after a restart harmless.
- **A job is an agenda item of kind "job"**, so it lives on the one clock, and the helper's work is a run with its own saved final state. `delegate` waits up to two and a half minutes for the result to come back in the same turn; past that, or when asked not to wait, the job carries on and Atlas reports its result as its own message when it ends, the way it delivers reminders. In a voice turn it doesn't wait unless the model asks to, so nobody sits in silence. The helper holds the job's lease for its budget plus ten minutes, on the one clock: if the lease runs out and the job hasn't ended (a restart it didn't come back from, a call that never arrived), the job ends as failed, with the reason, and the owner is told like any other result. A result nobody was waiting for stays marked until Atlas has said it, so a restart in between doesn't lose it. A restart doesn't fail a helper's run; its fiber carries on. A finished helper's own storage is deleted.
- **Production needs no new binding for helpers**: only the person agent is a Durable Object, and the helper class is exported from the Worker by name. The test runtime needs a test-only binding to host sub-agents (`core/vitest.config.ts`).
- **Areas are tagged by Workers AI's Clef** (`@cf/cloudflare/clef`), the `decide` role, after each run in the background, so tagging never slows a reply. Each owner message is asked one yes-or-no question per area, plus whether it's about something ongoing at all; an area is tagged at 0.6 or above, and below that the message gets no area (§6.3). Atlas's reply takes its question's areas, and a record takes the areas of the message it was learned from.
- **New areas are found, not set up.** A message that fits no area but is about something ongoing is set aside. Once there are five, the main brain groups them and names areas; an area that covers at least three of them is started, those messages are tagged, and the thread says so once ("Atlas started an area: Gym"). Clef can't name things, which is why the brain does that part. A set that made no area isn't sent again until five more messages are set aside; an area the owner archived or merged away isn't brought back, and a name that already exists takes the messages quietly. An item Clef refuses or doesn't answer within 20 seconds is left without an area, and tagging moves on; Clef reads the first 2,000 characters.
- **Corrections teach.** Moving an item's areas moves Atlas's reply, the records learned from it, or the records linked to it, and the item becomes an example for the new area and a counter-example for the old one. Each area's question to Clef carries its latest examples.
- **The hat is the scope, or else the conversation's current area.** A run wears the area the owner focused on with the scope switcher; without one, the areas of the latest tagged message in the last half hour. That costs no time before the reply. The run is told the area, its tone, and the names of all areas, and can still talk about any of them.
- **M1's per-area setting is tone.** The settings that control what leaves Atlas (accounts, sharing, faces, autonomy, private) come with the outbox in M2 and faces in M5.
- **The character is a preset, four scales and the owner's words** (§23.1, and the locked Character & voice screen). Presets are Atlas, Butler, Coach, Friend and Quiet; the scales are warmth, humor, directness and initiative, 1 to 5. Each becomes a plain sentence at the end of the character text, so changing them changes the stable block, never a filter on what Atlas says. The base text no longer says "warm" itself; the warmth scale does.
- **The character eval is old Atlas's, ported**: the same sixteen prompts and yardstick (`docs/CHARACTER.md`), the same pattern checks, and a judge that is always a different brain from the one judged (from the main and voice chains together). Atlas answers with no tools in the eval, and the judge is told so. It runs from Settings and from `npm run eval:character` against a deployment, and writes nothing to the log.
- **Passkeys, with the owner secret as the setup code.** On a fresh deploy, whoever opens the URL first could otherwise claim it, so the secret chosen at deploy (the Deploy button asks for it, so no terminal is needed) is required once, to add the first passkey. After that the owner signs in with a passkey. The setup code still gets them back in if every passkey is lost, and still works as a bearer token for scripts like the benchmark. WebAuthn is checked with SimpleWebAuthn (`@simplewebauthn/server` 14.0.3), which runs on WebCrypto; the tests drive it with a real P-256 authenticator. A passkey only works on the deployment it was made on.
- **Sign-ins are records the agent keeps, so they can be ended.** The cookie holds a random session id signed with the setup code; the agent keeps each session for 30 days. Signing out ends that session, removing a passkey ends every session it opened, and "Sign out everywhere" in Your data ends them all. Open sockets for an ended session are closed at once. Adding a passkey when one exists takes a sign-in from the last ten minutes, or the setup code, so a borrowed browser can't add its own.
- **The setup code is guarded.** It must be at least 16 characters (a shorter one is refused with the reason on the sign-in page), every check is constant-time, and after five wrong guesses each try waits longer, doubling up to 15 minutes. Posts from another site are refused. Sign-in asks the browser for any of this Atlas's passkeys without listing them, so a stranger learns nothing about them.
- **Passkeys don't require user verification.** Atlas asks for it ("preferred") but accepts a security key without a PIN, because the passkey is already bound to this deployment and the device. Requiring it would lock out keys that can't verify.
- **Atlas keeps a copy of the iCloud calendar, and signs in far less.** Every CalDAV request carries the Apple ID and password, so each one is a sign-in, and M1's pages read the calendar on every view; with several calendars that was hundreds of sign-ins a day from Cloudflare's shared addresses, the likeliest reason Apple kept refusing, even fresh passwords. The copy holds a week back through ten weeks ahead. A read within a minute of the last check answers from it; after that, one request lists every calendar's change tag, and only calendars that changed are read again. Ranges outside the copy are read directly.
- **A refusal no longer stops the calendar.** When iCloud refuses or is down, reads answer from the copy (up to three days old) and say how old it is; `calendar_read` hands the brain `as_of` and the reason. Atlas tries iCloud again by itself after 30 minutes, then 2, 6, 12 and 24 hours, never in a burst, and the refusal clears on the first success. Test and new credentials still try at once. Only after about a day of refusals does Atlas suggest a new app-specific password.
- **iCloud gets the password in Apple's own form** (lowercase, xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx) however it was pasted, and a User-Agent naming Atlas. A refusal's Worker log line keeps Apple's request id, partition and whether Apple asked for a password again (it doesn't when it's limiting sign-ins rather than rejecting the password), so the next refusal can be told apart.
- **Setup connects iCloud only, for now.** VISION §22 step 4 also names Google and Microsoft calendars and email. One-click sign-in with them needs an OAuth app registered for each deployment, which isn't one click for someone self-deploying, and Atlas doesn't read mail yet. They come with the senses in M4 and email in M5; the setup step says so.
- **Atlas's first look is an ordinary run** with the calendar and recall, asked to propose areas, key people and three to five things worth knowing, as JSON the setup screen shows. Each proposal is one tap to keep; kept areas are created like any other, and kept people become person records.
- **The UI follows the locked v1 screens** (docs/ui/) with what M1 has behind them. Home has Now (the ASCII diamond, Next, Needs you, Jobs, the week's load), Week, Jobs and any receipt opened onto the stage, with the console below. The other five places and settings each have their composer. Pages that would show things M1 doesn't have yet (approvals, automations, files, faces) say so plainly instead of showing samples.
- **Inbox holds what's waiting on you in M1:** finished background work to look over, and replies that didn't finish. Approvals and questions join with the outbox in M2. Items stay until the owner marks them done.
- **Themes and motion are per device**, in the browser's storage, and follow the system's light or dark setting until one is picked (§21.9). The theme is set before the first paint, so there's no flash.
- **Pinned holds areas in M1**; clicking one focuses on it. People, projects and automations join when they have pages.
- **The composer shows the area in focus**, not a guess made before sending: tagging happens after the run, in the background, so a guess would cost a call before every message. The message's own chip appears once it's tagged, and can be moved.
- **A refresh mid-answer picks up where the answer was**: the snapshot now carries what each run in progress has streamed so far.
- **⌘K finds places and records, or asks Atlas; ⌘J opens the transcript; Space talks.** The phone gets a tab bar (Home, Inbox, Agenda, Library, More) in place of the sidebar.
