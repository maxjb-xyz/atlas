**The stage** (§21.6)
1. "What's going on with Keyframes?" The Keyframes area opens on Home's stage as its own tab: what it is, its people, its plans and what was said about it lately. The console has a "Showing · Keyframes" receipt that opens it again.
2. "Who's Jordan?" puts Jordan on the stage: what Atlas knows, where it learned it, their areas, and what they're linked to. Clicking a person inside an area view opens them in place.
3. "Put my reminders this week in a table, with when and what." A table arrives on the stage with a line saying where it came from. "List what we decided about Riverside" arrives as a list. Neither holds anything Atlas didn't look up.
4. By voice: "show me Keyframes." The area opens on the stage while Atlas answers out loud in a sentence or two, without reading the view aloud.
5. Library → Areas → Open shows the same page as the stage view, and Library → People opens a person the same way. Correcting a record while its view is open updates the view without a reload.
6. A view asked for from another page arrives in that page's exchange as a small card that opens it on Home's stage. The transcript (⌘J) shows the same card.
7. Every view works in Graphite and Daylight, at desktop and phone width (a wide table scrolls inside its sheet, never the page), and with reduced motion.

**The agenda** (§4.3)
8. "Remind me to call the landlord if he hasn't replied by Friday." A receipt says what Atlas promised. Agenda shows it as a promise with the diamond, linked to where it was said. On Friday it happens, or, if you told Atlas he replied, it says so and doesn't.
9. "Check in with me about the Riverside quote tomorrow afternoon." Atlas makes a follow-up in the same step; there's no promise without a receipt. Tomorrow afternoon it asks.
10. "Never mind the landlord thing." The promise is cancelled, Agenda drops it, and History shows both the promise and the cancel. Cancel in Agenda does the same.
11. Redeploy while a promise is due. It still fires, once, after the restart.

**Automations** (§13)
12. "Every weekday at 8, give me a short brief." Atlas fills in an automation you can see and change. It starts paused; Test run shows what it would send and sends nothing; once on, it arrives at 8 on weekdays and not at weekends.
13. Saying "goodnight" runs an on-request automation's fixed steps, fast, with no model call: its run in History shows no brain.
14. Run on an on-request automation's card runs it from the Automations page.
15. An event trigger: moving an event on the calendar wakes the automation once, with the change. *(calendar)*
16. A watch on a web page wakes Atlas only when the change matters: History shows the quiet checks, and the one that mattered.
17. An absence trigger ("tell me if I haven't logged a workout since Monday") fires when the thing didn't happen, and stays quiet when it did.
18. The Automations page shows each automation as a When → Do → Report flow, with its run history (result, time and cost). An automation you make by hand in the form starts on; one Atlas made starts paused.
19. An automation that fails twice in a row tells you; after several more it pauses itself and says why.
20. After you've asked for the same thing three times, Atlas suggests an automation at the top of Automations.

**Approvals** (§12)
21. Settings → Connections → Webhooks: add one with a name, its address and a line on what it does ("Lights off", a Home Assistant webhook, or a test address from webhook.site). Call it now calls it, with a receipt in History.
22. "Turn off the lights." Atlas doesn't call it yet: an approval card opens on the stage and waits in Inbox, showing what will happen (Lights off, at its host, with any message), why it needs your OK, when it expires, and the checks it passed, ending in "Your OK". Approve calls it, and the receipt says what the webhook answered.
23. "Turn off the lights and delete the old Riverside note for good." One card covers both steps. Because one of them can't be undone, the card takes hold to confirm, with a bar that fills.
24. The same approval can be given from Inbox, from the push notification's Approve button (on Android and computers; on an iPhone, tapping the notification opens Inbox), by saying "yes, do it", or by replying "yes" in text. A message edited in the card is what's sent. Something that can't be undone is approved only by holding in the app; asked by voice, Atlas says it's waiting in Inbox.
25. After a delete for good, Library doesn't have the record, recall can't find it, and its old values are gone; History keeps that something was deleted, not what it said. Forget, with Undo, stays the everyday way.
26. Declining says so in the thread. An approval left waiting expires, and the thread says what didn't happen. Meanwhile, asking something else gets an answer: nothing waits behind it.
27. After five approvals of the same webhook without changes, Inbox asks whether Atlas can call it without asking. Saying yes stops the asking, and Settings → Permissions shows the whole table, the change, and a way to undo it. Deletes always ask.

**The outbox and notifications** (§4.4, §14)
28. Settings → Notifications → Turn on notifications here, on the phone (from the Home Screen on an iPhone) and on the computer. Send a test shows on that device. A reminder that fires while Atlas is closed everywhere arrives as a push on the device you used last, and only there; tapping it opens Atlas.
29. Quiet hours hold non-urgent things until they end; something urgent still comes through.
30. Each message goes to one channel: with the app open and in view, there's no push as well.
31. Every delivery leaves a receipt in History: the channel, the checks it passed, and when. A secret in something about to leave is caught, and it waits with the reason instead of going out.

**Default routines and Inbox**

35. Inbox lists approvals and questions beside finished jobs and failed replies, and empties as you act on them.

**Failure paths**

39. Refresh while an approval is waiting: the card is still there, and so is its countdown.

## 3. Deploy it

The same as M1 ([M1.md §3](M1.md#3-deploy-it)): `npm run deploy`, on Workers Paid (§6). Nothing migrates: a view lives in the log, on the receipt of the `show` call that made it, and the key that signs push notifications is made on the first start.

**Notifications.** In Settings → Notifications, press Turn on notifications here on each device that should get them. On an iPhone or iPad, add Atlas to the Home Screen first (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and turn them on from the app opened there; Safari in a tab can't take them.

**Webhooks.** In Settings → Connections → Webhooks, add one with its name, its https address, and a line on what it does; Atlas reads that line to know when to use it. For Home Assistant, use a webhook trigger in an automation and its address through your Nabu Casa or other public URL: Cloudflare can't reach a home network directly. For a first try, a free address from webhook.site shows each call as it arrives.

**Things to check on the account.** These couldn't be checked from the build sandbox.

## 5. Decisions made while building

- **A view is a receipt.** `show` checks the view against its contract and saves it on its own `tool.result` row in the log, with a "Showing · …" receipt. The console's receipt opens it, Home's stage opens it as its own tab when it arrives, and a refresh still has it, because the log is the truth (§4.1). Nothing else stores views until Keep (§21.7).
- **One contract per view, shared.** Each view's zod schema is in `core/src/stage.ts`. The model fills it as `show`'s input, the agent saves it, and the app renders from the same type (§19.4). The input is one object, `{ view: … }`, so every provider gets a plain object at the top of the tool's parameters.
- **Area and person views point at what Atlas knows; list and table carry their content.** An area or person view holds only which one (the model may name it; `show` saves the id). The app asks the agent for that page's live data, so a view opened tomorrow shows tomorrow's records, and a correction updates an open view. A list or table is what Atlas put together in that turn, so it carries its rows, with a line saying where they came from.
- **The area page and the area view are one thing** (§21.2). Library → Areas → Open and Library → People open the same components the stage shows, in Library's sheet. That also covers the "Detail pages" docs/ui lists as not drawn yet, for areas and people; they follow the locked shell's parts (cells, rows, chips, mono labels) until they get their own design pass.
- **Compact and expanded.** Expanded is the view on a sheet. Compact is a small card for places with less room: an exchange on a page other than Home, and the transcript. It names the view, shows a few lines, and opens it on Home's stage.
- **Only the owner's runs can show.** Runs Atlas starts itself (reminders, job reports) have no one looking at the stage when they run, so they don't get `show`; the clock's runs and helpers can't reach it.
- **One channel, one device** (§14). Each open app tells Atlas whether it's in view (the tab is showing). A message to the owner goes to the app when one is in view, out loud when they're in a call, and otherwise as a push to the device used last, falling back to the next if that one fails. VISION says reading a message on one device clears it everywhere; sending to one device in the first place keeps that true without silent pushes, which browsers penalize (Safari drops a subscription that gets them).
- **What can wait, and what can't.** Replies to something the owner just asked and reminders they set go out at once (they chose the time). Everything else Atlas starts, such as a job's report, waits through quiet hours. Held notifications are agenda items of kind `notify` on the one clock, and when quiet hours end they go out together as one push, unless the owner has opened Atlas meanwhile. Focus, the interruption budget and urgency chosen per message come with automations, which are the first things to need them.
- **Receipts for everything beyond the thread.** A push, a held push, a webhook call, and anything stopped at the exit each write a `delivery` row: where it went, what was checked, what was sent, and on failure the provider's status, host and own words. History shows them; the thread doesn't.
- **Push is the Web Push standard, signed by this deployment.** Atlas makes its own VAPID key pair on first start and keeps it with the secrets; payloads are encrypted for each device (RFC 8291) with WebCrypto, so nothing is added to the Worker. A device the push service says is gone (404 or 410) is forgotten, and the receipt says so.
- **The rule is code, by kind** (§12.1, `ASKS` in `core/src/approvals.ts`). A tool declares its kind: looking, changing the owner's own things, reaching outside, or can't be undone. Tools that reach outside or can't be undone never act themselves: they hand their action to the way out, which does it at once if the kind and earned trust allow, or adds it to the run's approval card. Only the outbox carries out an approved action.
- **One card per plan, on the one clock.** Every action a run asks for joins that run's card, and the card is an agenda item of kind `approval`, so it expires on time (12 hours) and the clock says what didn't happen. Its answer (approved, declined, ran out) is a line in the thread, and the next run's context lists what's waiting and what was settled in the last two hours, so Atlas never says something is done that isn't.
- **Where a yes can come from.** Inbox, the card on Home's stage, a push notification's Approve button (through `/api/approve`, with the session cookie), and the `approve` tool when the owner says yes by voice or in text. Anything that can't be undone takes only a hold in the app: a spoken or typed yes isn't enough, and the push has no Approve button for it. An edit in the card is what goes out, and an edited approval doesn't count toward trust.
- **Earned trust per webhook.** Five approvals of the same webhook in a row without edits, and Inbox asks whether Atlas can stop asking for it. A decline or an edit starts the count again. Settings → Permissions shows the whole table and what Atlas no longer asks about, with Ask again. Nothing that can't be undone is ever trusted.
- **Not yet:** each area raising or lowering these defaults (§12.1), and showing a card for money (nothing spends yet). Per-area autonomy joins Library → Areas with the other settings that control what leaves Atlas.
- **Webhooks are kept with the secrets.** Their addresses are credentials, so the app and the model only see names and hosts, and the scrubber keeps addresses from leaving. A POST sends `{ text, from, at }`. A call's receipt keeps its status, the host and, on failure, the service's own words.
- **Deleting for good erases the record's values from the log too.** The record, its history, its links and its place in recall go, and the log's record changes and the tool calls that saved it are blanked; open apps get the blanked rows at once. The log keeps that something was deleted. The owner's own message it came from stays, since that's the conversation (§4.2).
- **A reminder says; a promise does.** A reminder is the owner's words at a time. A promise is something Atlas said it would do later: check on something, follow up, remind only if something hasn't happened. Both are agenda items on the one clock, made in the same step with a receipt and Undo. A promise carries Atlas's note to itself (what to look at, what decides it, what to tell the owner) and the owner's words that led to it.
- **Keeping a promise is a run of Atlas's own.** When it comes due, Atlas runs with its note and the owner's words, with the tools to look (recall, the calendar), to make or cancel reminders and promises, and nothing that saves facts about the owner (§5.4). It always tells the owner how it went, and if no brain answers, the owner still hears what was due and that Atlas couldn't look into it.
- **One tool to list and one to cancel.** `agenda_list` and `agenda_cancel` (which replaced `reminder_list` and `reminder_cancel`) cover reminders and promises alike, and every run's context lists both with their ids, so "never mind the landlord thing" finds it.
- **Fires once, even across a restart.** Taking the lease on a due item also sets a wake for when the lease runs out. If a restart cuts off the run keeping it, the wake fires it again; if it was kept, the wake finds it done and does nothing.
- **Agenda shows what's Atlas's.** Reminders and promises carry the diamond, with "You said, …" and the owner's words underneath, and Cancel. Approvals wait in Inbox and held notifications are the outbox's, so neither shows in Agenda or on the Week.
- **One object, one contract.** An automation's trigger, steps and report are zod schemas (`AutomationInput`) shared by Atlas's `automation_save`, the form and the store. Webhook steps name a webhook; the run looks it up, so a renamed or removed webhook fails with the reason instead of calling the wrong thing.
- **Its next run is an agenda item.** Each automation on a schedule, a watch or an absence has one agenda item of kind `automation`, re-armed after every run (also after a failed one, so a bad night doesn't stop tomorrow's). Agenda shows it; pausing takes it off the clock. The others run on a phrase, Run, or another automation finishing.
- **A phrase runs without a model.** When the whole message is exactly an automation's phrase (case and punctuation aside, `$inputs` filled in), its steps run in place of a reply. Fixed steps need no model, so "goodnight" is fast and its run shows no brain; anything else goes to Atlas as usual. Only automations that are on answer to their phrase. This is matching the owner's own words to a phrase they set, not filtering what the model says (rule 1).
- **Atlas's start paused.** What Atlas makes, and any change it makes, waits for a test run before it can be turned on; one made in the form starts on. Turning one on grants the webhooks in its steps; an Atlas step that wants any other webhook still asks.
- **A test run sends nothing.** It uses real data, runs Atlas steps with tools that can only look, and webhooks only say what they'd do. For a watch or an absence it also does the look and says what it found.
- **Watches look before they wake Atlas** (§10.3, §13.2). The page is fetched and reduced to its words; if it changed, Clef is asked whether the change is the kind that matters, and Atlas runs only on a yes. The first look, no change, and a change that doesn't matter are each a quiet row in History.
- **Absences check by keyword, once per stretch.** At the set time, Atlas searches what was said since the window began; nothing found runs the automation, and it won't run again until the thing happens and stops happening again. Matching by meaning can join it once embeddings are on every deployment.
- **Failures count, and tell.** A run that fails says so where the automation reports. Two failures in a row tell the owner; five pause it with the reason, shown on its page.
- **Discord waits for M5.** A first try made Discord a post-only bot, to give approvals something to guard. That isn't what Discord should be: as in Hermes and OpenClaw, it's a channel you talk to Atlas through, which VISION §16.1 places with the other channels in M5. It was taken out rather than left half there.
