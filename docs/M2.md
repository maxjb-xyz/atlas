# M2: follow-through

**Status:** started 2026-10-22. M1 is merged to `main`. Built so far: the stage's `show` tool with the area, person, list and table views, and the outbox for real: one channel per message, push notifications, quiet hours, delivery receipts, and Discord as the first way out to other people (§2). The owner's decisions are in §6. [HANDOFF-M2.md](HANDOFF-M2.md) has where M1 left things.

M2 proves that everything Atlas says it'll do actually happens (VISION §25). It adds:

- **the stage's `show` tool, first** (§21.6): area, person, list and table from what Atlas already knows, then draft, approval, plan and automation as their features land, and Keep (§21.7) last;
- **the agenda** (§4.3): promises and follow-ups as agenda items, made in the same step Atlas makes them;
- **automations** (§13) with all five triggers, test runs, paused starts, run history and suggestions;
- **approvals with earned trust** (§12);
- **the outbox and notifications** (§4.4, §14), with push notifications to the owner's devices;
- **the default routines:** a morning brief, an evening wrap-up and a weekly review;
- **Inbox** with approvals and questions.

**Exit:** the smoke test (§1) passes on the owner's deployment, then M1's and M0's.

## 1. The smoke test

Written before the build (VISION rule 7), from the draft in [HANDOFF-M2.md §6](HANDOFF-M2.md#6-a-first-draft-of-m2s-smoke-test). Run it on the owner's deployment after every deploy that touches the core, then M1's list, then M0's. Each line should just work. When one doesn't, the fix goes in the character, the context, a tool's description or the tool (rule 1), and the finding goes in §4.

Steps marked *(calendar)* need a calendar Atlas can read (§6, item 2). Steps marked *(Discord)* need the Discord bot connected (§3).

**The stage** (§21.6)
1. "What's going on with Keyframes?" The Keyframes area opens on Home's stage as its own tab: what it is, its people, its plans and what was said about it lately. The console has a "Showing · Keyframes" receipt that opens it again.
2. "Who's Jordan?" puts Jordan on the stage: what Atlas knows, where it learned it, their areas, and what they're linked to. Clicking a person inside an area view opens them in place.
3. "Put my reminders this week in a table, with when and what." A table arrives on the stage with a line saying where it came from. "List what we decided about Riverside" arrives as a list. Neither holds anything Atlas didn't look up.
4. By voice: "show me Keyframes." The area opens on the stage while Atlas answers out loud in a sentence or two, without reading the view aloud.
5. Library → Areas → Open shows the same page as the stage view, and Library → People opens a person the same way. Correcting a record while its view is open updates the view without a reload.
6. A view asked for from another page arrives in that page's exchange as a small card that opens it on Home's stage. The transcript (⌘J) shows the same card.
7. Every view works in Graphite and Daylight, at desktop and phone width (a wide table scrolls inside its sheet, never the page), and with reduced motion.

**The agenda** (§4.3)
8. "Remind me to call the landlord if he hasn't replied by Friday." A receipt says what Atlas promised. Agenda shows it as a promise with the diamond, linked to where it was said. On Friday it happens, or, if you told Atlas he replied, it says so and doesn't.
9. "Check in with me about the Riverside quote tomorrow afternoon." Atlas makes a follow-up in the same step; there's no promise without a receipt. Tomorrow afternoon it asks.
10. "Never mind the landlord thing." The promise is cancelled, Agenda drops it, and History shows both the promise and the cancel. Cancel in Agenda does the same.
11. Redeploy while a promise is due. It still fires, once, after the restart.

**Automations** (§13)
12. "Every weekday at 8, give me a short brief." Atlas fills in an automation you can see and change. It starts paused; Test run shows what it would send and sends nothing; once on, it arrives at 8 on weekdays and not at weekends.
13. Saying "goodnight" runs an on-request automation's fixed steps, fast, with no model call: its run in History shows no brain.
14. Run on an on-request automation's card runs it from the Automations page.
15. An event trigger: moving an event on the calendar wakes the automation once, with the change. *(calendar)*
16. A watch on a web page wakes Atlas only when the change matters: History shows the quiet checks, and the one that mattered.
17. An absence trigger ("tell me if I haven't logged a workout since Monday") fires when the thing didn't happen, and stays quiet when it did.
18. The Automations page shows each automation as a When → Do → Report flow, with its run history (result, time and cost). An automation you make by hand in the form starts on; one Atlas made starts paused.
19. An automation that fails twice in a row tells you; after several more it pauses itself and says why.
20. After you've asked for the same thing three times, Atlas suggests an automation at the top of Automations.

**Approvals** (§12)
21. Anything that speaks to someone else waits for your OK, as one card for the whole plan: the exact content, where it goes and who sees it, why it needs your OK, and the checks it passed, ending in "Your OK". *(Discord)*
22. "Move the shoot reminder to Thursday and tell the studio channel." One card, one tap, covers both steps. *(Discord)*
23. The same approval can be given from Inbox, from a push notification, by saying "yes, send it", or by replying "yes" in text when it's the only one waiting. An edit made in the card is what goes out. *(Discord)*
24. Something that can't be undone needs hold to confirm, with a bar that fills.
25. An approval left waiting expires, and Atlas tells you what didn't happen. Meanwhile, asking something else gets an answer: nothing waits behind it.
26. After five approvals of one kind without changes, Atlas offers to stop asking. Saying yes stops it, and the Permissions page shows the whole table, the change, and a way to undo it. *(Discord)*

**The outbox and notifications** (§4.4, §14)
27. Settings → Notifications → Turn on notifications here, on the phone (from the Home Screen on an iPhone) and on the computer. Send a test shows on that device. A reminder that fires while Atlas is closed everywhere arrives as a push on the device you used last, and only there; tapping it opens Atlas.
28. Quiet hours hold non-urgent things until they end; something urgent still comes through.
29. Each message goes to one channel: with the app open and in view, there's no push as well.
30. Every delivery leaves a receipt in History: the channel, the checks it passed, and when. A secret in something about to leave is caught, and it waits with the reason instead of going out.
31. Settings → Connections → Discord: paste the bot's token. The test says the bot's name and the channels it sees, and the invite link adds it to a server. Send a test message posts it there, with a receipt in History. A wrong token says so in Discord's own words. *(Discord)*
32. Settings → Notifications shows channels by urgency, quiet hours, focus and the interruption budget, and a change applies to the next message.
33. With notifications blocked in the browser, Settings → Notifications says so, and messages wait in the app.

**Default routines and Inbox**
34. The morning brief, evening wrap-up and weekly review are on the Automations page, editable, and each arrives on time with real content: the calendar, reminders and promises. *(calendar)*
35. Inbox lists approvals and questions beside finished jobs and failed replies, and empties as you act on them.

**Failure paths**

37. No brain answers when an automation runs: its history shows the failure with the reason, and the report says it didn't run.
38. A push subscription that's gone (a browser removed) is dropped after its first failure, with the reason in History.
39. Refresh while an approval is waiting: the card is still there, and so is its countdown.

## 3. Deploy it

The same as M1 ([M1.md §3](M1.md#3-deploy-it)): `npm run deploy`, on Workers Paid (§6). Nothing migrates: a view lives in the log, on the receipt of the `show` call that made it, and the key that signs push notifications is made on the first start.

**Notifications.** In Settings → Notifications, press Turn on notifications here on each device that should get them. On an iPhone or iPad, add Atlas to the Home Screen first (Share, then Add to Home Screen) and turn them on from the app opened there; Safari in a tab can't take them.

**Discord.** Make a bot for Atlas at [discord.com/developers/applications](https://discord.com/developers/applications): New Application, name it, then Bot → Reset Token, and copy the token. In Settings → Connections → Discord, paste it and press Connect. Use the "Add the bot to a server" link to invite it, then Test again: it lists the channels it can see. The bot needs no privileged intents.

**Things to check on the account.** These couldn't be checked from the build sandbox.

| What | Where | What to check |

## 5. Decisions made while building

- **A view is a receipt.** `show` checks the view against its contract and saves it on its own `tool.result` row in the log, with a "Showing · …" receipt. The console's receipt opens it, Home's stage opens it as its own tab when it arrives, and a refresh still has it, because the log is the truth (§4.1). Nothing else stores views until Keep (§21.7).
- **One contract per view, shared.** Each view's zod schema is in `core/src/stage.ts`. The model fills it as `show`'s input, the agent saves it, and the app renders from the same type (§19.4). The input is one object, `{ view: … }`, so every provider gets a plain object at the top of the tool's parameters.
- **Area and person views point at what Atlas knows; list and table carry their content.** An area or person view holds only which one (the model may name it; `show` saves the id). The app asks the agent for that page's live data, so a view opened tomorrow shows tomorrow's records, and a correction updates an open view. A list or table is what Atlas put together in that turn, so it carries its rows, with a line saying where they came from.
- **The area page and the area view are one thing** (§21.2). Library → Areas → Open and Library → People open the same components the stage shows, in Library's sheet. That also covers the "Detail pages" docs/ui lists as not drawn yet, for areas and people; they follow the locked shell's parts (cells, rows, chips, mono labels) until they get their own design pass.
- **Compact and expanded.** Expanded is the view on a sheet. Compact is a small card for places with less room: an exchange on a page other than Home, and the transcript. It names the view, shows a few lines, and opens it on Home's stage.
- **Only the owner's runs can show.** Runs Atlas starts itself (reminders, job reports) have no one looking at the stage when they run, so they don't get `show`; the clock's runs and helpers can't reach it.
- **One channel, one device** (§14). Each open app tells Atlas whether it's in view (the tab is showing). A message to the owner goes to the app when one is in view, out loud when they're in a call, and otherwise as a push to the device used last, falling back to the next if that one fails. VISION says reading a message on one device clears it everywhere; sending to one device in the first place keeps that true without silent pushes, which browsers penalize (Safari drops a subscription that gets them).
- **What can wait, and what can't.** Replies to something the owner just asked and reminders they set go out at once (they chose the time). Everything else Atlas starts, such as a job's report, waits through quiet hours. Held notifications are agenda items of kind `notify` on the one clock, and when quiet hours end they go out together as one push, unless the owner has opened Atlas meanwhile. Focus, the interruption budget and urgency chosen per message come with automations, which are the first things to need them.
- **Receipts for everything beyond the thread.** A push, a held push, a Discord post, and anything stopped at the exit each write a `delivery` row: where it went, what was checked, what was sent, and on failure the provider's status, host and own words. History shows them; the thread doesn't.
- **Push is the Web Push standard, signed by this deployment.** Atlas makes its own VAPID key pair on first start and keeps it with the secrets; payloads are encrypted for each device (RFC 8291) with WebCrypto, so nothing is added to the Worker. A device the push service says is gone (404 or 410) is forgotten, and the receipt says so.
- **Discord is a bot the owner makes.** The token is a secret (and the scrubber keeps it from leaving); what the bot can see is kept as a copy, read again by Test. Posts never ping anyone (`allowed_mentions` is empty). The owner can send a test message from Settings; Atlas itself gets a tool to post once approvals guard it (§12), so nothing speaks to other people unasked in the meantime.
