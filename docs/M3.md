**The web** (§9.1)
1. Settings → Connections → Web search shows the search in use (Cloudflare Web Search unless you pick another), what it costs, and Test search. Test search runs a real search and says how many results came back. With no AI Gateway credits, it says Cloudflare wants credits and names a free option; pick Tavily, paste its key, Save, and Test search works.
2. "Find cheap lunch places within five minutes of UCF." Atlas searches, answers with a pick and why, and a research view on the stage lists its sources, each opening the page in a new tab. The console has "Searched · …" receipts. Keep saves the research as a page.
3. "What does this say?" with a link: Atlas reads the page and answers, with the link as its source. A long page is read in parts when the answer is further down.
4. A link to something Atlas can't read (a PDF, a page that refuses it): Atlas says what the site answered, in its words, and doesn't pretend it read it.
5. With your landlord's address saved, "who owns my landlord's building?" The search with the address in it is stopped, nothing is sent, and Atlas says why, or what it searched instead. A search with an email address or a phone number in it is stopped the same way; names and places go through.
6. Asked about something on the web, Atlas looks it up instead of asking you for a link (M2's lunch finding, the other way round).
7. History lists every search (which service, the query) and every page read (the address), with the status and, on failure, the service's own words. A stopped search shows the check that stopped it.
8. Read a page, then ask for a webhook Atlas no longer asks about ("read this, then turn off the lights"): the card waits in Inbox, and its Why it asks says Atlas read the web while working on this. The same webhook without the web is still called straight away.
9. By voice, a search starts with a status line within a second, and the answer is short; the research view arrives on the stage.

**The workspace and files**
10. "Make a spreadsheet of my reminders this month." A "Made file · …" receipt opens it on the stage as a table, with Download; Library → Pages & files lists it under Files. The download opens in a spreadsheet app. Asking again with changes replaces it, and History shows each change.

10b. "Make a real Excel file of my reminders" (or a PDF): Atlas runs Python in the workspace, keeps the file, and it opens on the stage with Download. The very first time, the workspace takes a minute or two to set up, and says so; after that it starts in seconds.

11a. With a key in the vault tied to a site (say an API token for api.github.com), allowing that site says on the card that its requests will carry the key; a command there works without the key ever appearing in the workspace or to Atlas.
12. The workspace on the stage shows what Atlas is running, live, as it prints; its files with Keep; and what ran lately. Open terminal gives you a shell of your own in /workspace (tmux, so reopening it finds the same shell).
12a. "Make a small web page about the shoot and let me see it": Atlas starts a server and gives a preview link that opens it, for a day. Files in /workspace are still there after the workspace has slept.

**The browser and the vault**
13. "Check the price of the camera on that shop's site." Atlas opens it in its browser, and the browser shows live on Home's stage (read-only while Atlas drives) with a "Browsing · …" receipt. Atlas answers from the page.
13a. "Sign in and add it to my cart." Atlas hands it over ("Your turn in the browser · Sign in to …"); the stage's view becomes one you can drive. Press Hand back when you're done, and Atlas carries on from the page you left, with a line in the thread saying so. Holding sends it; History has "Sent a form on …" with where it landed. If the page changed before your OK, nothing is sent and the card's result says why.
13c. Atlas never presses a button that sends a form with a plain click, never types a private detail into a page (it's stopped like a search), and sees a password field only as "(filled)".
14. Settings → Vault: add a login by name ("Shop login"), tied to its site, with its username and password (and a two-factor seed if the site has one). The page shows the name, site and which fields are set, never a value.
14a. "Sign in to the shop." Atlas fills the login and presses Sign in as one step. The first time on that site it waits on a card (Fill in Shop login on …, which fields, then the button); after your yes it signs in and carries on, and next time it signs in without asking. Settings → Vault shows the site under "Fills without asking on", with Ask again.
14b. Asked to use the login on another site, Atlas says it only fills on its own. A card in the vault asks every time, with a hold.
14c. Atlas never sees a value: its answer and History name the fields, a page that greets you by your username shows it to Atlas as [hidden], and a password or card number can't leave in a message. History has "Filled in Shop login on …, then pressed “Sign in”" for each use.

**Code Mode, skills and finding a way**
15. "Compare these five lunch places: price, distance and hours" runs as one program (a "Ran a program · N calls" receipt), with each search's own receipt beside it, faster than step by step. Something in a program that asks (a webhook, a form) still waits for your OK.

16a. Settings → Skills → Paste a SKILL.md from Claude Code or another agent: it's listed, Atlas finds it in its index, and Download SKILL.md gives it back unchanged. A skill with a check (`metadata: atlas-check`) is re-checked weekly in the workspace; one that breaks is flagged there and in Atlas's index, and you hear once.
17. Ask for something whose first route fails (a price on a shop page that refuses Atlas's reader, a package that won't install): Atlas tries another route (its browser, another search, a few lines in the workspace) before asking, and asks only for what only you can give (a password, a decision, money). When it's truly stuck, it sends one message: what it tried, and the one thing it needs.

**Connected services (MCP)** (§9.2, §21.6)

## 3. Deploy it

The same as M2 ([M2.md §3](M2.md#3-deploy-it)): `npm run deploy`, on Workers Paid. Nothing new is made on first start; settings that don't have a web search yet read as Cloudflare Web Search.

**Web search.** Cloudflare Web Search runs on the Workers AI binding the deployment already has, through the account's `default` AI Gateway, and is paid from AI Gateway credits (Cloudflare dashboard → AI → AI Gateway → Credits; about $0.25 per 1,000 searches with the default provider, and no free credits). For a free allowance instead, pick Tavily (1,000 searches a month, no card), Brave ($5 of searches a month, card needed) or Exa ($10 a month) in Settings → Connections → Web search, paste its key, and Save.

**Files.** The Worker binds an R2 bucket, `atlas-files`, as `FILES`. R2 has to be turned on for the account once first: Cloudflare dashboard → R2 Object Storage, and accept its terms (the free allowance needs no payment, but the page may ask for a card on file). Then `npm run deploy` creates the bucket itself (Wrangler's automatic provisioning). Until R2 is on, Cloudflare refuses Wrangler's look at the account's buckets with a 403, and the deploy fails. `npx wrangler r2 bucket list` shows Cloudflare's own words for why. The first start after the deploy adds the files table.

**The workspace.** The Worker now has a container application, `Workspace`, on the Durable Object scheduling policy (a public beta), and a second Durable Object class (migration `v2`). It starts from Cloudflare's managed `cloudflare/debian-trixie` image, so `npm run deploy` needs no Docker. The first command after the deploy sets the workspace up (installs Python and the rest, a minute or two) and keeps that as a snapshot; later starts take seconds. Snapshots last 30 days unused; after that the next start sets up again, and says so. Container time is billed while it runs and for the ten minutes it waits before sleeping (a `basic` instance: ¼ vCPU, 1 GiB); light personal use stays inside what Workers Paid includes.

**Code Mode.** The Worker binds a Worker Loader as `LOADER` (Dynamic Workers, on Workers Paid; $0.002 per unique program a day past the first 1,000 a month). Nothing to set up.

**The browser.** The Worker now binds Browser Run as `BROWSER` (`core/wrangler.jsonc`); it needs Workers Paid, which the deployment has. Nothing to set up. Each browser stays open for ten minutes without use, then closes; Browser Run bills by browser time.

## 5. Decisions made while building

- **Search and reading are looks, and they leave Atlas.** They never ask (§12.1), but a query or an address goes to someone else's server, so the tools reach the web only through the outbox's `look` (§4.4). It runs the secrets scrubber and the public-query check first; something private stops it with the reason, nothing is sent, and Atlas gets the reason to act on. Either way a `delivery` row with channel `web` keeps the service, the query or address, the status and, on failure, the service's own words.
- **The public-query check is about details, not topics** (decided with the owner). It stops a secret, an email address, a phone number, or a street address that's in Atlas's records. Names, places and topics go through: a person's name alone isn't treated as private, so "Dana Whitfield Orlando" is a fine search but her saved address isn't. Addresses in a page's address bar are checked decoded, so `%40` is still an email.
- **Atlas reads the web itself, and the exit gets stricter** (decided with the owner; VISION §20 records it). A page's text and a search's results come back to Atlas marked as someone else's words. Once a run has read the web, itself or through a helper it started, anything that leaves Atlas in that run asks first, even a webhook Atlas had earned trust for, or one an automation was allowed to call. The approval card's Why it asks says so. Long or many reads still belong to helpers (§8.1), which can search and read but can't act.
- **The readable part of a page.** `fetchPage` takes a page's `<main>` or `<article>` when that holds most of its words, else the body without its header, nav and footer, keeping headings and list items. At most 16,000 characters come back at once, with `more_from` to read on; a PDF or an image says what it is, since Atlas can't read those yet. At most 4 MB of a response is read. Watches keep their own `pageText` comparison unchanged, so a deploy doesn't make every watched page look changed.
- **Cloudflare has a search after all.** The handoff said Cloudflare had no search API; it opened Cloudflare Web Search on 2026-10-02, on the Workers AI binding (`env.AI.websearch`), with Ceramic, Exa or Linkup behind AI Gateway. The Agents SDK's own `web_search` and `web_fetch` tools weren't used: Atlas's tools go through its outbox and log (rule 3), and its page reader keeps the watch's comparison.
- **One interface, four searches** (decided with the owner). Cloudflare Web Search is the default because it needs nothing beyond the deployment, but it isn't free, so the owner can pick a service with a free allowance instead. Each is one case of `search` in `core/src/web.ts`, mapping the service's answer to title, address, snippet and age. Their keys are secrets: kept apart, never sent to the app, and caught by the scrubber. A date filter Cloudflare can't apply comes back as a note rather than being dropped silently.
- **Everyone who looks can look at the web.** The owner's runs, Atlas's own runs (keeping a promise), automations' Atlas steps, test runs (looking sends nothing to people) and helpers all have `web_search` and `web_fetch`.
- **Narrow tools, not a program, for the browser.** The Agents SDK's browser module gives the model raw CDP inside a program; the approval rule couldn't tell a click from "Place order" there. Atlas's browser has five tools instead, and only `browser_submit` sends a form. A click on a button marked `submits` (a form's submit button) is refused with the reason. The SDK's module also needs `@cloudflare/codemode`, which comes with Code Mode; Atlas's own CDP client is about a hundred lines.
- **Sending a form can't be undone** (VISION §12.1 lists submit). It waits on a card with hold to confirm, like a delete. The outbox sends it, and only if the browser is still on the page it was asked on and the button still says the same; otherwise nothing is sent and the card's result says why. Because opening a page counts as reading the web, everything that leaves in a browsing run asks anyway.
- **The owner drives logins.** `browser_take_over` hands the browser to the owner with a reason; the view becomes one they can drive, and Atlas's browser tools refuse until they hand it back. Hand back is a message from the owner ("I'm done in the browser. It's on … Over to you."), so Atlas carries on in an ordinary run. The owner can also take it over unasked; giving that back doesn't start a run.
- **What Atlas sees of a page.** Its readable text (the same reader as `web_fetch`) and up to 150 numbered elements: links with their addresses, buttons, fields with their values, lists with their options. Password, card and one-time-code fields only say whether they're filled. What Atlas types passes the public-query check, like a search.
- **One browser, ten minutes.** One Browser Run session per person, kept between calls and closed by Browser Run after ten minutes without use. Each tool call connects, works, reads the page and lets go. Live links last about five minutes; the view asks for a new one every four.
- **The vault is built in, first** (VISION §15). Entries are kept with the other secrets in the agent's own storage, which Cloudflare encrypts at rest; the app and the model only get names, kinds, sites and which fields are set. A login can hold a two-factor seed, and the code is made inside the vault when it's filled (RFC 6238, checked against its test values). 1Password and Bitwarden, and codes texted to Atlas's own number, come later.
- **Filled at the last step, by the outbox.** `vault_fill` names the entry, which of its fields go into which of the page's fields, and optionally the button to press after, so a login is one step. The outbox fills them only if the browser is still on that page and its site is one the entry is tied to (`chase.com` also covers `secure.chase.com`, never `chase.com.evil.example`). Atlas gets back "Filled 2 fields from … and pressed …".
- **When it asks** (§12.1). The first use on each site waits on a card; the owner's yes is remembered for that site, shown in Settings → Vault with Ask again. A card asks every time, with a hold, since money always asks. A site already said yes to fills even in a run that read the web: the entry can't go anywhere but its sites, so a page can't steer it (VISION §20 records the exception).
- **Atlas never sees a value.** Every vault value, usernames and names on cards included, is hidden from what Atlas reads back (pages, fields, links, titles) as [hidden]. Only the secret ones (passwords, card numbers and codes, keys, seeds) are kept out of what leaves Atlas, so Atlas can still say the owner's name.
- **After a yes, Atlas carries on.** A yes in the app (or a push) to a browser or vault step is followed by a message from the owner, "Yes to … Carry on.", so a login or a form in the middle of a task doesn't leave the task hanging. A yes said by voice or in text is already part of a run, which carries on by itself.
- **Files are bytes in R2, and a row here.** A file's name, type, size and who made it are a row in the agent's storage; its bytes are in R2 under its id. Saving a name that's already there replaces it in place, and every make, replace and delete is a `file.change` row in History. Atlas makes text files (CSV, Markdown, HTML, JSON, calendar files, code) with `file_save`; anything else comes out of the workspace.
- **Downloads are the owner's, and sandboxed.** `/api/files/:id` needs the owner's sign-in, sends the file as an attachment, and adds `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox` so an HTML or SVG file Atlas made from web content can't run scripts as Atlas. PDFs go without it, since Chrome won't show a PDF in a sandbox and a PDF can't script the page.
- **The workspace is a Sandbox, the 1.0 way** (VISION §19.3). Cloudflare's Sandbox runs untrusted code in a container (a Firecracker microVM with its own kernel and network). Sandbox SDK 1.0 (2026-09-30) has no ready-made class: the sandbox is your own Durable Object driving `ctx.container`. That's `Workspace`. The `@cloudflare/sandbox` package isn't used: its 1.0 helpers need a binary from Cloudflare's sandbox Docker image, which would mean Docker for every deploy; plain commands on the managed image do the same jobs.
- **Set up once, kept as a snapshot.** The managed image has Node only. The first start installs Python 3 (pandas, openpyxl, python-docx, reportlab, matplotlib, requests), git, curl, jq, sqlite3, tmux and a few more from Debian, then snapshots the container; later starts restore it. Changing the package list starts a new snapshot.
- **/workspace outlives the container.** Containers lose their disk when they sleep. After each command, /workspace is packed into R2 (up to 200 MB; past that, the run says it isn't kept), and unpacked when a new container starts.
- **No internet but approved sites.** The container starts with no internet; every HTTP and HTTPS request goes through `WorkspaceEgress`, a Worker entrypoint with the allowed sites in its props. A site not allowed gets a 403 that says so, and is reported to Atlas as `blocked`, so it asks for that one with `workspace_allow`. The owner's yes is remembered (subdomains included) and listed on the workspace view, with Remove. The gate re-signs HTTPS, so the container trusts its certificate on every start.
- **Keys are added outside.** A vault key tied to an allowed site is added to that site's requests by the gate (`Authorization: Bearer …`, or the value as given if it has a scheme). The container, its code and Atlas never see it (§15).
- **Commands, streamed.** `workspace_run` runs bash in /workspace with a time limit (two minutes by default, up to fifteen); what it prints goes to the app as it prints, and the result keeps the start and end of the output. The view keeps the last twelve runs.
- **A terminal of your own.** The workspace view's Open terminal connects xterm.js, over a WebSocket through the Worker (signed in, from the app's own page), to tmux in a PTY. Opening the terminal starts the workspace; looking at the view doesn't.
- **Previews are links, not the app's origin.** A server Atlas starts in the workspace gets a link at `/preview/<token>/`, good for a day for anyone who has it, like Cloudflare's own preview URLs. It's served with `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox`, so the page runs as its own origin and can't reach Atlas with the owner's sign-in. A server that uses absolute paths for its files won't find them under the prefix; that's a known limit.
- **Code Mode is Sandbox's other half.** Cloudflare Sandbox runs code in a container or a Dynamic Worker; the workspace is the first, Code Mode the second, with `@cloudflare/codemode`'s executor (no AI SDK needed). `code_run` takes an async arrow function; inside it each of the run's own tools is `atlas.<tool>(input)`, except `code_run` itself and `delegate`. Each call goes through the tool: its own input schema, its kind and approvals (a webhook in a program waits on the run's card like any other), and its receipt, logged as a row of the run marked `via: code`. The program has no network (`globalOutbound: null`): the web is `atlas.web_search` and `atlas.web_fetch`, which go through the outbox. At most 60 calls and five minutes.
- **Skills are SKILL.md, kept in Atlas's storage** (§9.3). A skill is its frontmatter's name and description plus its Markdown body, stored as rows with every version (like pages). Skills from other agents are pasted in as SKILL.md and come back out unchanged; Atlas's own check rides in `metadata: atlas-check`. Skills that bring their own scripts (a folder) aren't supported yet: only the SKILL.md.
- **Found again.** Every active skill's one line is in each run's context, most used first; `skill_read` opens one and counts the use, and a skill whose last check failed says so in the index. The character says to open a skill before a task it covers.
- **Learned from outcomes, by Atlas's judgment.** There are no reactions to read yet (thumbs come later), so the character and `skill_save`'s description say when: after a task that took working out and that the owner was happy with. The receipt is "Learned a skill · …" with Undo (back a version, or archived if it was the first). Atlas's own runs (promises, automations) can read skills but not write them.
- **Checked weekly, on the one clock.** Once a skill has a check, an agenda item of kind `skills` runs every check in the workspace each week, flags failures, and tells the owner once per skill that broke. It isn't shown in Agenda: it's upkeep, not something of the owner's.
- **Tidied, never deleted.** Skills unused for four months are archived by the memory tidy-up, which runs after messages; using one brings it back.
- **Finding a way is character, not a mechanism** (§9.2). There are no step limits to raise (§4.1), so persistence is a paragraph in the character: try another route first, ask only for what only the owner can give, and when stuck send one message with what was tried and the one thing needed. The honesty paragraph no longer says to stop at the first failure. The yardstick has the same line, so the character eval judges it.
- **MCP through the Agents SDK's client.** It connects (Streamable HTTP or SSE), signs in with OAuth and keeps servers and tokens in the agent's storage across sleeps; Atlas only adds its side. A sign-in comes back to `/mcp/callback` on the app's own address, which the Worker hands to the agent without the owner's cookie (a link from another site can't carry a SameSite=Strict cookie); the SDK accepts only a sign-in it started, by its state. Success goes back to Settings; a failure shows the service's own words.
- **Tools are found, not listed.** One server can have a hundred tools, so they aren't in every prompt. Each run's context names the connected servers (what each says it's for, how many tools, or that it's waiting for a sign-in), and Atlas finds tools with `mcp_tools` and uses one with `mcp_call`. Code Mode's programs reach them the same way.
- **When a service's tool asks** (§12.1). A tool its server marks read-only is a look. Anything else reaches outside: it asks until trusted (five yeses as it was, per tool, like a webhook), and one its server marks destructive always asks, with a hold. A tool with no annotations asks until trusted: MCP's own default would call it destructive, which would put a hold on every unannotated read. What it sends is shown on the card as JSON, and can be edited there. A service's answer is someone else's words, so a run that read one asks before anything leaves, like a run that read the web (§20).
- **Atlas can ask to connect a server** (§9.2: growing its own abilities). `mcp_add` makes a card with the address and why; on a yes it's connected, and one with a sign-in waits for the owner. Only the owner can give a token, in Settings.
- **MCP Apps in one sandboxed frame.** The spec asks web hosts for a second origin for the page; Atlas has one address, so the page runs in a frame with `sandbox="allow-scripts allow-forms"` (no same-origin, so it's an origin of its own and can't reach Atlas or the sign-in) under a Content-Security-Policy built from what its server declared (only well-formed domains; nothing by default). A page that needs its own storage or same-origin features won't get them. Camera, microphone and the like aren't granted.
- **What a page may do.** It gets its tool's input and result once it's initialized, can call tools its server lets pages use (visibility includes `app`) on its own server only, read that server's resources, open web links in a new tab, and follows the app's theme. It can't send messages as the owner or change what Atlas knows: `ui/message` and `ui/update-model-context` aren't offered. A page's own calls are the owner's actions, like a button in Settings, so they don't ask; each leaves a receipt. A tool only for pages isn't offered to the model. The last twenty pages are kept to show again.
- **Extensions wait** (§9.2, §17). Growing abilities is MCP servers and skills for now. Writing an extension (code Atlas adds to itself) needs the extension format VISION §17 describes, and that's a milestone of its own; nothing in M3's smoke test depends on it.
- **Charts follow one method.** Bars or lines, one axis, at most eight series, colors in a fixed order from a palette checked for color blindness in both themes (`--s1` to `--s8`). Two or more series get a legend; up to four lines are also labelled at their ends, pushed apart so they don't overlap. Every chart has a hover tooltip and a Table view. Bars start at zero; lines fit their data. A long unit ("min/mi") is said once above the axis, not on every tick. Charts are drawn at the size they're shown, so the type stays the same size on a phone and a wide screen.
- **Numbers that don't line up are refused, with why.** A series with the wrong number of values, a comparison row with the wrong number of cells, or a pick that isn't one of the options comes back to Atlas as an error naming it, inside a kit too, so it fixes the view rather than the app guessing.
- **Small apps run with nothing.** An app is one HTML document in a frame with `sandbox="allow-scripts"` (no same-origin, so no sign-in and no Atlas) and a Content-Security-Policy that allows nothing outside the page. Its data comes in as `window.ATLAS_DATA`, the theme's colors as CSS variables, and the frame follows the page's height.
- **Receipts in the console.** "Searched · the query" and "Read · the page's title" show what Atlas looked at; the research view shows what it concluded and from where.

## 6. Decided with the owner

1. **The calendar (2026-10-22):** keep waiting. The web comes first; the calendar steps stay open as regression until the owner has run the browser test in [HANDOFF-M2.md §3.1](HANDOFF-M2.md#31-icloud-refuses-atlass-sign-in).
