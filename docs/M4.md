# M4: senses

**Status:** built 2026-10-22, waiting for its smoke test on the owner's deployment. Everything in M4's scope is built (§2) and run locally (§4), but nothing yet on Cloudflare, on a phone or on a Mac: §7 lists what only the deployment can show. [HANDOFF-M4.md](HANDOFF-M4.md) has where M3 left things.

M4 proves **the gym-to-store moment, and others like it** (VISION §25): Atlas notices what's happening and speaks up when it matters, and only then. The gym is one example, not the design: whatever its senses notice (a move, a change on the calendar, traffic, the weather, a device) gets the same three steps, and what to make of it is Atlas's own judgment. It adds:

- **location as a sense** (§10.1): OwnTracks posting to Atlas, arrivals and departures at saved places, sparse events in the log;
- **the situation** (§10.2): a small live record in every prompt, rebuilt from events, never guessed;
- **decide-then-wake awareness** (§10.3): rules, then `decide`, then Atlas only when it might matter; rhythm moments that may end in silence; reactions to nudges logged with what each was about;
- **errands** Atlas keeps in mind as you move about, at any kind of place;
- **nodes** (§16.3): the protocol, and the desktop app as the first node;
- **M4's stage views** (§21.6): situation, map, place and weather, and Now and day rebuilt from the situation.

**Exit:** the smoke test (§1) passes on the owner's deployment, then M3's carried steps and the earlier lists.

## 1. The smoke test

Written before the build (VISION rule 7), from the draft in [HANDOFF-M4.md §6](HANDOFF-M4.md#6-a-first-draft-of-m4s-smoke-test) and the owner's decisions (§6). Run it on the owner's deployment after every deploy that touches the core, then the regression lists. When a line doesn't just work, the fix goes in the character, the context, a tool's description or the tool (rule 1), and the finding goes in §4.

Maps are OpenStreetMap's free services unless Settings → Connections → Maps says Mapbox. Steps marked *(Mapbox)* need a Mapbox token there.

**Location** (§10.1)
1. Settings → Devices → Location → Set up OwnTracks shows Atlas's address, a user and a password made for OwnTracks alone, and a setup link (and its QR code) that opens OwnTracks on the iPhone with all of it filled in. After one tap, OwnTracks reports to Atlas, and Settings → Devices shows the last fix: when, how accurate, the phone's battery.
2. Settings → Connections → Maps shows OpenStreetMap in use, with nothing to set up, and Test, which looks up an address and a route and says how it went (or the service's own words when it doesn't). Library → Places: a place with an address gets a pin; "this is my gym" while you're there pins the gym where you are, with a "Pinned · Gym" receipt and Undo.
2a. Switching Maps to Mapbox and pasting a token: Test says it works, travel times now include traffic, and pins already made stay as they are. *(Mapbox)*
3. Saved places with pins show in OwnTracks as regions on the phone, without setting them up there.
4. Arriving at the gym and leaving it each show once in History ("Arrived at Gym", "Left Gym, after 1 h 10 min"), not a line per fix. Moving about away from saved places adds a line only when you've really moved somewhere, not every few minutes.
5. A wrong password, or OwnTracks posting after its password was made again, is refused (401), and Settings → Devices says when the last refused post came. Nothing else on the Worker answers to OwnTracks' password.
6. Turning location off in Settings → Devices stops it at once: what OwnTracks posts after that is dropped, History says location was turned off, and "where am I?" gets "I don't know: location is off" rather than a guess from the last fix. Turning it back on starts again from the next fix.

**The situation** (§10.2)
7. "Where am I and what's next?" answers from the situation, without a lookup: the place (or "out, 3 km from home"), since when, the next thing on the calendar and when to leave for it. With no fix for a while, Atlas says how old its last one is.
8. With an event that has a place later today, the situation has the travel time from where you are and a leave-by time with a margin (more with OpenStreetMap, which has no traffic); it changes as you move, and Atlas never says a travel time it didn't get. With Mapbox it follows traffic. *(Mapbox for traffic)*
9. "Do I need an umbrella this afternoon?" answers from the weather in the situation (Open-Meteo, for a rough area around you, never your exact spot). History shows the weather looks, about one an hour while Atlas is in use, not one per message.
10. The situation includes what you're doing (the event under way, focus) and your devices (the phone's battery and how it's connected; a paired Mac and whether it's online). A run never shows coordinates to you unless asked.

**Awareness** (§10.3)
11. "I need oat milk." Atlas keeps it as an errand (an "Errand · Oat milk" receipt; Agenda lists it under Errands, with Done). Leaving the gym, a nudge arrives within a minute or two: a store a few minutes off your way home, by name, with how far off the way it is, and whether you still need oat milk. An errand at any kind of place works the same ("get flowers for Mom" finds a florist).
11a. Others like it, with nothing set up for them: a meeting moved or added on the calendar, traffic that means leaving earlier, rain coming before a walk to your next thing. Each shows in History as noticed (Calendar, Traffic, Weather), then decided, and Atlas speaks up when it would help and stays quiet when it wouldn't. The same change isn't news twice.
12. Each reaction to a nudge is logged with what it was about: a reply, "thanks", "stop", and ignoring it (after two hours). History shows each nudge with its reaction, and Atlas's next looks see how the last ones landed. "Stop" makes Atlas work out what you meant to stop, as narrow or broad as you said it, and change Speaking up with a receipt and Undo: Settings → Notifications → Speaking up shows it under Don't bring up (or the moment's switch off), where you can change it back.
13. Events that don't matter end quietly: arriving home with nothing going on shows in History as decided not to matter (decide's "worth a thought" score with it), in under a second, and nothing woke the main brain (no run, no cost on the Brains page).
14. A rhythm moment (start of day) looks at the day and either says something useful (one message, a push if the app isn't open) or stays silent; History shows it ran and which it chose. It never says the same thing twice in a day, and it waits for quiet hours to end.
15. A calendar event with a place: the nudge to leave comes about ten minutes before the leave-by time from real travel time ("Leave by 2:35 for the dentist: about 22 minutes"), once, even if the situation changes after.
16. Settings → Notifications → Speaking up has a switch for what Atlas notices, one for leaving for events, the start of day and evening with their times, and Don't bring up, a list you can add to and remove from. Each one turned off stays quiet, and "move my morning look to seven" changes it from the chat.
17. A nudge with the app open shows in the thread; with it closed, it's a push; in a call, it's said aloud. Each one counts against the day's interruption budget like any push that could wait.

**Nodes** (§16.3)
18. Publish a release on GitHub (or run the Desktop app workflow by hand), and download Atlas for Apple silicon from it. macOS asks once before opening it (it isn't signed: right-click → Open, or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open anyway). It lives in the menu bar, with its status.
19. Its first screen asks for your Atlas's address, then shows a QR code and a short code. Scanning it with your phone (signed in to Atlas), opening it in the Mac's browser, or typing the code in Settings → Devices asks "Pair this device?", naming the Mac. After Pair, the Mac opens Atlas in its window, and shows in Settings → Devices as online with its tools: notify, open a link (which asks first), and run a command, which is sensitive and off, with a switch. Remove unpairs it at once, and the app asks to pair again.
20. "Show a notification on my Mac saying hi" works while the Mac is online ("On your Mac · Notified" receipt). Turning on running commands and asking for one ("what's using my disk on my Mac?") asks first, on a card naming the Mac and the command.
21. With the Mac asleep or the app closed, Atlas says so ("Your Mac's offline. I'll do it when it's back."), and when it's back the call runs and Atlas tells you how it went. The Mac's online and offline show in History.

**The views** (§21.6)
22. "Show me where the dentist is" puts a map on the stage with the place marked, and a place view with its address, what Atlas knows about it, and your visits (from arrivals). Both read well in light and dark and on a phone, with OpenStreetMap's credit on the map.
23. "How do I get to the dentist from here?" puts the route on a map, with the travel time and leave-by if it's on the calendar.
24. "What's the weather today?" puts a weather view on the stage: now, the next hours, and what matters (rain, heat, wind).
25. Home's Now is rebuilt from the situation: where you are and since when, what's next with leave-by, and weather only when it matters. "Show me my situation" puts the situation view on the stage, the same parts in full.

**Regression**
26. M3's carried steps ([M3.md §7](M3.md#7-carried-into-m4)): the public-query check stopping a private detail (5), a run that read the web asking before a webhook (8), search by voice (9), files and the workspace (10, 10b, 11a, 12a), the browser and the vault (13 to 14c), Code Mode and skills (15, 16, 16a), finding a way (17), compare, the kit and a small app (17b, 17c), MCP Apps (17f), a server sign-in coming back connected (17d), and stopping a turn (17i).

## 3. Deploy it

`npm run deploy`, as before. The first start adds nothing to the tables; settings without the new parts read as their defaults (location on, OpenStreetMap, units from the time zone, Speaking up with start of day at 08:00 and evening off).

**OwnTracks.** Install OwnTracks on the iPhone. In Atlas, Settings → Devices → Set up OwnTracks, then scan the QR code with the phone's camera (or open the link on the phone). OwnTracks asks to import the configuration; say yes, and allow location Always. It reports in significant-changes mode (light on the battery), with your saved places as regions. The password is shown once; setting up again makes a new one.

**Maps and weather.** Nothing to set up: OpenStreetMap's services and Open-Meteo need no key. For traffic in travel times, make a free Mapbox account, copy its default public token, and paste it in Settings → Connections → Maps and weather after picking Mapbox. Test maps and weather looks up two places, a route and the weather, and says how it went.

**The desktop app.** On GitHub, publish a release (any tag, like `desktop-v0.1.0`). The Desktop app workflow builds Atlas for Apple silicon on a Mac runner (about 10 to 15 minutes; on a private repo that's about ten times as many of the free minutes) and attaches `Atlas_0.1.0_aarch64.dmg` to the release. Running the workflow by hand keeps the `.dmg` as the run's artifact instead. Open it, drag Atlas to Applications, and open it once with right-click → Open.

**Things to check on the account.** These couldn't be checked from the build sandbox.

| What | Where | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| OwnTracks reaching the Worker | `/owntracks`, `run_worker_first` in `core/wrangler.jsonc` | The first post shows a fix in Settings → Devices. A 401 in OwnTracks' status means its user or password don't match; anything else (a 404 or an HTML page) means the route didn't reach the Worker. |
| OpenStreetMap from Cloudflare | `core/src/maps.ts` | Test maps and weather works. Nominatim, FOSSGIS and Overpass could refuse Cloudflare's shared addresses (a 403 or 429); the error says so and suggests Mapbox. |

Clef now also scores "worth a thought" for every event: if it says yes to ordinary moves (too many wakes on the Brains page) or no to a moved meeting, that's the finding, and the words in `worthQuestion` and `#openThings` are the fix. |
| The desktop app's window | `open_main` in `desktop/src-tauri/src/lib.rs` | Atlas opens signed in after the setup code. A passkey may not work inside the app's window (it's a web view, not Safari); the setup code always does. |
| Notifications on the Mac | `notify` in `desktop/src-tauri/src/node.rs` | The first one asks to allow notifications from Atlas. |

## 4. Found while building

These came from running M4 locally: `wrangler dev` with a fresh state folder and no AI binding, the scripted brain patched in (never committed), OwnTracks' posts by curl, Chromium through Playwright, and the real desktop node built on Linux under a virtual display.

- **The Worker served its static files before OwnTracks could reach it.** `run_worker_first` listed `/api`, `/agents`, `/preview` and `/mcp` only, so on Cloudflare `/owntracks` (and `/nodes`) could have been answered by the app's files. Both are listed now. Tests didn't show it: they call the Worker directly.
- **The voice layer already sends a `welcome` message on every socket.** A device's first message collided with it, so every node message type now starts with `node.`.
- **OpenStreetMap works from a Worker:** Nominatim, FOSSGIS's OSRM and Open-Meteo answered a local Worker through the sandbox's proxy ("a test route took 3 min, 17°C"). Open-Meteo once answered 503 (a connection timeout); the error said so, and the next look worked. Nominatim didn't find "UCF Recreation and Wellness Center"; Atlas says what it searched, and "this is my gym" while there works.
- **Pinning a place where you are didn't mark you there** until the next fix; it does now.
- **A move was taken as a duplicate** of any move in the last ten minutes, since moves have no place. Duplicates are for arriving at or leaving the same place now; moves have a twenty-minute cool-down instead, and leaving a place always counts.
- **The setup test's rare failure** ([HANDOFF-M4.md §3.4](HANDOFF-M4.md#34-smaller-items)): it waited for any settings view, and one an earlier step left unread could arrive before the people were saved. Waits now name the view they expect.
- **A review of the whole M4 change found ten more**, each traced and fixed with a test where the code allows:
  - A paired device's socket went through the voice layer, which answers a typed turn or a call itself, so a device could have started a turn as the owner. Device sockets now go around it, can't start a call, and get none of the SDK's own messages.
  - Start of day and evening were moved to tomorrow whenever the agent woke cold for them, which is most mornings. A moment that's due is left for the clock now.
  - A call to a device skipped the public-query check, and opening a link didn't ask; both do now. The card shows the whole command, and trust on a device is earned per exact command, not for every command.
  - Pair again in the desktop app left the old socket open; its tasks now end with it. A device's newer socket closes its stale one.
  - A queued call could be stuck forever after a restart mid-run, or queued for a device already removed; neither now.
  - A nudge held for quiet hours counted as ignored before it arrived; its two hours start when it's released.
  - An errand's deadline could leave the errand stuck if the reminder failed, and a leave look could be finished twice.
- **Awareness was built too narrowly at first** (the owner, after reading the build). It took only moves and places, woke Atlas only for an open errand, reminder or commitment, scripted the errand case ("find a place on the way home"), and sorted nudges into five fixed kinds that "stop" switched off. The gym was VISION's example, not the design. Now every sense event goes through the same steps; `decide` also asks whether it's worth a thought at all; the situation's own changes are senses; Atlas's note and the character ask for judgment rather than a script; nudges say what they're about in Atlas's words; "stop" is Atlas's to interpret, into a Don't bring up list the owner can see and edit; and a place can be any kind a map knows.
- **The real node worked end to end:** paired through the app's Pair page, a notification ("Shown."), a command after its card's yes ("exit 0, hello from the node"), reconnecting by itself after the Worker restarted, and a call that waited while the app was closed and ran when it came back.

## 5. Decisions made while building

- **The map is always OpenFreeMap's tiles,** drawn with MapLibre in the app and loaded only when a map is shown, whichever maps provider is picked. Mapbox changes routes and store search, not the map, so its token never needs to reach the browser.
- **Pairing is the device flow.** The device shows the QR code and the owner scans it signed in, not the other way round, since a Mac has no camera pointed at a phone. The code works ten minutes, at most five wait at once, and a token is handed over once.
- **Sensitive and acting are separate.** A capability that's sensitive starts off; one that acts asks first, with earned trust per device and tool like a webhook. Running a command is both.
- **Awareness is judgment, not a script.** Code decides only what's cheap and fixed (a switch is off, the same news twice, a move within twenty minutes of the last look) and what `decide` is asked. What an event means, and whether to speak, is Atlas's: the character says how it pays attention, and the note says what happened and what it may touch. Fixing a nudge that's wrong means changing those words (rule 1).
- **Awareness speaks with a tool, not its last words.** A run of Atlas's own ends with a note for the log; only `nudge` reaches the owner, so silence is simply not calling it, and the run still ends replied.
- **The situation never waits on the network.** It reads a cache the refresh keeps current, so a fresh calendar event may take until the next refresh (a turn, a sense event or the tick) to show; the calendar tool still reads it at once.
- **What Atlas notices is one switch,** not one per kind: kinds would be guesses about what the owner cares about. Don't bring up holds the owner's own words instead, which both Atlas and `decide` read.
- **Start of day is on, evening off,** by default. Each look may stay silent and sees what Atlas already said today, so it doesn't repeat the morning brief if that's on.
- **Errands sit on the clock with no time** unless they have a deadline, which reminds once and leaves the errand open until it's done.
- **Where M5 can replace before adding:** the calendar trigger's polling in `core/src/agent/automations.ts` and the situation's calendar refresh read the same events; one should feed the other. Automations' event trigger could take `sense` rows (arriving at a place) instead of growing another kind.

## 6. Decided with the owner

1. **The calendar (2026-10-22):** iCloud answers again, so it stays the calendar; the calendar steps of earlier milestones are back in the regression lists.
2. **Location (2026-10-22):** OwnTracks over HTTPS to start (a web app can't send location in the background). VISION §10.1 records it.
3. **Maps, routes and places (2026-10-22):** first Mapbox; then, since it isn't free for kept pins and needs a key, OpenStreetMap's free services by default, with Mapbox's free tier as a choice in Settings. VISION §19.1 records it, and §4.4 lets places go to the maps services and nowhere else.
4. **Weather (2026-10-22):** Open-Meteo, with no key, for a rough area. VISION §19.1 and §4.4 record it.
5. **The first node (2026-10-22):** the Tauri desktop app, built sixth in M4's order (after location, the situation, awareness and errands), for Apple silicon, unsigned, built by GitHub Actions only when a release is published (or by hand), to save build minutes: Mac runners count ten times on a private repo, and the repo goes public later. VISION §16.3 records it.
