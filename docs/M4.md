# M4: senses

**Status:** started 2026-10-22. The smoke test (§1) is written before the build; nothing in it is built yet. [HANDOFF-M4.md](HANDOFF-M4.md) has where M3 left things.

M4 proves **the gym-to-store moment, and others like it** (VISION §25): Atlas notices what's happening and speaks up when it matters, and only then. It adds:

- **location as a sense** (§10.1): OwnTracks posting to Atlas, arrivals and departures at saved places, sparse events in the log;
- **the situation** (§10.2): a small live record in every prompt, rebuilt from events, never guessed;
- **decide-then-wake awareness** (§10.3): rules, then `decide`, then Atlas only when it matters; rhythm moments that may end in silence; reactions to nudges logged;
- **errands** matched to where you are and your way home;
- **nodes** (§16.3): the protocol, and the desktop app as the first node;
- **M4's stage views** (§21.6): situation, map, place and weather, and Now and day rebuilt from the situation.

**Exit:** the smoke test (§1) passes on the owner's deployment, then M3's carried steps and the earlier lists.

## 1. The smoke test

Written before the build (VISION rule 7), from the draft in [HANDOFF-M4.md §6](HANDOFF-M4.md#6-a-first-draft-of-m4s-smoke-test) and the owner's decisions (§6). Run it on the owner's deployment after every deploy that touches the core, then the regression lists. When a line doesn't just work, the fix goes in the character, the context, a tool's description or the tool (rule 1), and the finding goes in §4.

Maps are OpenStreetMap's free services unless Settings → Connections → Maps says Mapbox. Steps marked *(Mapbox)* need a Mapbox token there.

**Location** (§10.1)
1. Settings → Devices → Location → Set up OwnTracks shows Atlas's address, a user and a password made for OwnTracks alone, and a setup link (and its QR code) that opens OwnTracks on the iPhone with all of it filled in. After one tap, OwnTracks reports to Atlas, and Settings → Devices shows the last fix: when, how accurate, the phone's battery.
2. Settings → Connections → Maps shows OpenStreetMap in use, with nothing to set up, and Test, which looks up an address and a route and says how it went (or the service's own words when it doesn't). Library → Places: a place with an address gets a pin; "this is my gym" while you're there pins the gym where you are, with a "Pinned · Gym" receipt and Undo.
2a. Switching Maps to Mapbox and pasting a token: Test says it works, travel times now include traffic, and pins already made stay as they are. *(Mapbox)*
3. Saved places with pins show in OwnTracks as regions on the phone, without setting them up there.
4. Arriving at the gym and leaving it each show once in History ("Arrived at Gym", "Left Gym, after 1 h 10 min"), not a line per fix. Moving about away from saved places adds a line only when you've really moved somewhere, not every few minutes.
5. A wrong password, or OwnTracks posting after its password was made again, is refused (401), and Settings → Devices says when the last refused post came. Nothing else on the Worker answers to OwnTracks' password.
6. Turning location off in Settings → Devices stops it at once: what OwnTracks posts after that is dropped, History says location was turned off, and "where am I?" gets "I don't know: location is off" rather than a guess from the last fix. Turning it back on starts again from the next fix.

**The situation** (§10.2)
7. "Where am I and what's next?" answers from the situation, without a lookup: the place (or "out, 3 km from home"), since when, the next thing on the calendar and when to leave for it. With no fix for a while, Atlas says how old its last one is.
8. With an event that has a place later today, the situation has the travel time from where you are and a leave-by time with a margin (more with OpenStreetMap, which has no traffic); it changes as you move, and Atlas never says a travel time it didn't get. With Mapbox it follows traffic. *(Mapbox for traffic)*
9. "Do I need an umbrella this afternoon?" answers from the weather in the situation (Open-Meteo, for a rough area around you, never your exact spot). History shows the weather looks, about one an hour while Atlas is in use, not one per message.
10. The situation includes what you're doing (the event under way, focus) and your devices (the phone's battery and how it's connected; a paired Mac and whether it's online). A run never shows coordinates to you unless asked.

**Awareness** (§10.3)
11. "I need oat milk." Atlas keeps it as an errand (an "Errand · Oat milk" receipt; Agenda lists it under Errands, with Done). Leaving the gym, a nudge arrives within a minute or two: a store a few minutes off your way home, by name, with how far off the way it is, and whether you still need oat milk.
12. Each reaction to a nudge is logged against its kind: a reply, "thanks", "stop", and ignoring it (after two hours). History shows each nudge with its reaction. "Stop" turns that kind of nudge off, and Settings → Notifications → Speaking up shows it off, where you can turn it back on.
13. Events that don't matter end quietly: arriving home with no errand open shows in History as decided not to matter, in under a second, and nothing woke the main brain (no run, no cost on the Brains page).
14. A rhythm moment (start of day) looks at the day and either says something useful (one message, a push if the app isn't open) or stays silent; History shows it ran and which it chose. It never says the same thing twice in a day, and it waits for quiet hours to end.
15. A calendar event with a place: the nudge to leave comes about ten minutes before the leave-by time from real travel time ("Leave by 2:35 for the dentist: about 22 minutes"), once, even if the situation changes after.
16. Settings → Notifications → Speaking up lists what Atlas may speak up about on its own (errands, leaving for events, start of day, evening), each with a switch and, for the moments, a time. Each one turned off stays quiet.
17. A nudge with the app open shows in the thread; with it closed, it's a push; in a call, it's said aloud. Each one counts against the day's interruption budget like any push that could wait.

**Nodes** (§16.3)
18. Download the desktop app for an Apple silicon Mac from the repo's releases. macOS asks once before opening it (it isn't signed); after that it opens the Atlas you point it at, signed in as you.
19. Settings → Devices → Pair a device, or the desktop app's own first screen, shows a QR code and a short code. Scanning it with your phone (signed in to Atlas), or opening its link, asks "Pair this Mac?", with what it offers. After Pair, the Mac shows in Settings → Devices with its capabilities, the sensitive ones (running commands, reading files) off, each with a switch. Remove unpairs it at once.
20. "Show a notification on my Mac saying hi" works while the Mac is online ("On your Mac · Notified" receipt). Turning on running commands and asking for one ("what's using my disk on my Mac?") asks first, on a card naming the Mac and the command.
21. With the Mac asleep or the app closed, Atlas says so ("Your Mac's offline. I'll do it when it's back."), and when it's back the call runs and Atlas tells you how it went. The Mac's online and offline show in History.

**The views** (§21.6)
22. "Show me where the dentist is" puts a map on the stage with the place marked, and a place view with its address, what Atlas knows about it, and your visits (from arrivals). Both read well in light and dark and on a phone, with OpenStreetMap's credit on the map.

## 2. The plan

How each part fits the one loop, one log, one clock and one way out (rule 3). §2 becomes "What's built" as parts land.

**Location is a sense, and a sense writes events.** OwnTracks posts to `POST /owntracks` on the Worker with HTTP Basic auth: a user and password made for it alone, kept with the secrets, and nothing else answers to them. The agent keeps the last fix in its state, and writes a `sense` row to the log only when something happened: an arrival, a departure, a move of more than a few hundred metres away from saved places, location turned off or on. Arrivals and departures come from one state machine on the server (inside a place's radius, or not), fed by both OwnTracks' own region events and plain fixes, so each shows once whichever arrives first. Saved places with a pin go back to the phone as OwnTracks regions in the answer to its next post (`setWaypoints`).

**Places get pins.** A place record keeps `lat`, `lon` and `radius_m` beside its address. Atlas pins one from its address through Nominatim, or where you are now (`place_pin`). Maps are one small interface (`core/src/maps.ts`) with two providers: OpenStreetMap's free services by default (Nominatim for addresses, FOSSGIS's OSRM for routes, Overpass for what's near a route, OpenFreeMap for the map), and Mapbox by key (Directions with traffic, Search Box category search along a route, its own map). Pins always come from Nominatim or the phone, since Mapbox's free answers can't be kept. Every maps and Open-Meteo call leaves through the outbox as a look, on a `maps` or `weather` channel, with a delivery receipt and Atlas's own User-Agent; the public-query check still stops secrets, emails and phone numbers, and places may go only to the maps services (VISION §4.4). The free services ask for at most one request a second and no heavy use, so answers are cached and calls are sparse. Weather gets coordinates rounded to a tenth of a degree.

**The situation is computed, not stored as truth.** `core/src/situation.ts` builds it from the last fix and the latest `sense` rows, the calendar (the event under way, the next one with a place), focus, the cached travel time and weather, and the devices. Travel time and weather are cached in state with when they were fetched, and refreshed on the clock only while they're needed (an event with a place in the next few hours; weather at most hourly while Atlas is in use). It goes in every prompt's fresh part, beside focus and reminders, in a few lines.

**Awareness is the clock and `decide`, not a second loop.** Every `sense` row goes through `core/src/awareness.ts`:
1. **Rules:** the kind is off in Speaking up, or a duplicate of one in the last few minutes. Ends there, logged.
2. **Decide:** Clef is asked, per open errand and per kind of nudge that's on, whether the event matters to it. Under the bar, it ends there, logged as not relevant with the scores.
3. **Atlas:** a run of Atlas's own, with the event, the situation and what it matched. It speaks up with the `nudge` tool or ends without it, which is silence. A nudge leaves through the outbox like every message, as something that can wait (quiet hours, focus and the budget apply), tagged with its kind.

Rhythm moments are agenda items (`rhythm`) on the one clock: start of day and evening at their times, and a leave-by look before each event with a place. Each is an awareness run that may end in silence, and sees what Atlas already said today.

**Reactions are logged per kind.** After a nudge, the owner's next message in the thread within half an hour is its reaction (a reply, "thanks" or "stop", told apart by `decide`); a push opened or dismissed is too; nothing for two hours is "ignored". Each is a `sense`-like row of its own (`nudge.reaction`), and the awareness run's note carries the recent tally for that kind. "Stop" turns the kind off in Speaking up, where the owner turns it back on.

**Errands are agenda items.** An `errand` has a title and optionally a place or a kind of place (Mapbox's category, like `grocery`), and no time unless it has a deadline. `errand_add` and `errand_done` are the tools; Agenda shows them under Errands. On a departure, Atlas finds stores along the route home: the route from OSRM, then Overpass for shops of that kind within a few hundred metres of it (or Mapbox's category search along a route), and the detour for the best few.

**Nodes dial out.** Pairing is the device flow: the node asks the Worker for a pairing code, shows it as a QR code and text, and the owner opens it signed in and taps Pair, which gives the node its own token. The node then holds one WebSocket to the person agent (`/nodes/connect`), says hello with its capabilities (tools with a JSON schema, whether each is sensitive, and the events it sends), sends events (which become `sense` rows), and answers calls. Atlas sees paired devices and their tools in the situation and uses them through one tool, `device_call`, like `mcp_call`. A tool that does more than notify asks first, through the approvals it already has. A call to a device that's offline waits on the agenda (`device` items) and runs when it's back.

**The desktop app** is Tauri, in `desktop/`: the web app in a window, a menu bar item, and the node in Rust (pairing, the socket, notifications, running a command when allowed). GitHub Actions builds it for Apple silicon on a Mac runner and attaches the `.dmg` to a release. It isn't signed yet.

**The views** are contracts in `core/src/stage.ts` like the rest: `situation`, `map` (OpenFreeMap's tiles drawn with MapLibre in the app, loaded only when a map is shown, with markers, a route line and the credit; with Mapbox, its own tiles through the Worker, so the token never reaches the browser), `place` and `weather`. Now on Home reads the situation.

**Size.** About 18,800 lines of core before M4, against 25k. M4's budget is about 2,000 lines of core; the desktop app is outside the core count, like the app.
