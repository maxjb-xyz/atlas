# M7: launch

**Status:** open. The smoke test (§1) was written before the build (VISION rule 7), from the draft in [HANDOFF-M7.md §6](HANDOFF-M7.md#6-a-first-draft-of-m7s-smoke-test) and the owner's choices of 2026-10-24 (§3). M7 is built in stages; each stage closes when its part of the smoke test passes on the owner's deployment, and the milestone closes with the last. **Stage 1 (the look) closed on 2026-10-24** with the owner's approval of the mockups (docs/ui §8).

M7 proves **someone else can install it and love it** (VISION §25). Before a public release, the app has to meet two bars, simplicity and Jarvis, and it has to do what people will compare it with Hermes and OpenClaw on. So beside the launch work HANDOFF-M7 planned (setup steps 6 and 7, moving in, all themes, ambient mode, docs, the deploy button and a release), M7 adds, all decided with the owner on 2026-10-24:

- **the new places and settings** (VISION §21.2): People and Abilities in the sidebar, Atlas settings down to five sections, services as cards, a live map of what Atlas can do, and every record clickable;
- **releases and updates** (§23.2): one-click update and rollback from the Updates page;
- **brains** (§18): Anthropic by API key, named brains for kinds of work, and what a job does when its brain runs out;
- **abilities** (§9.3, §17): one catalog of services from an index, extension tools, and skills learned by a review after each conversation;
- **jobs Atlas owns** (§8.3): any long piece of work, with checkpoints with the owner;
- **reach** (§12.3, §14, §16.1): where a message Atlas starts goes, the owner's own permission rules, and phone calls through Telnyx;
- **what Cloudflare can see** (§20): Atlas's own encryption of the vault and keys, and plain words about it in the docs.

**Exit:** every stage's part of the smoke test passes on the owner's deployment, then M6's carried steps and the earlier lists; then someone other than the owner installs Atlas from the README alone.

## 1. The smoke test

Run each stage's part on the owner's deployment when that stage is built, and again after every deploy that touches it. When a line doesn't just work, the fix goes in the character, the context, a tool's description or the tool (rule 1), and the finding goes in this doc.

### Stage 1: the look

1. Mockups of the changed screens are in [docs/ui/](ui/README.md), drawn in the locked visual language, in dark and light: the sidebar with eight places, Abilities (the map, Services, a service opened, Skills, Devices), People (Contacts, Members, Faces), and Atlas settings' five sections (Atlas, Brains, Notifications, Permissions, Account). The owner has approved them, and the approval is written in docs/ui's README with the date. (This stage's check is the owner's OK, not the deployment.)

### Stage 2: groundwork

2. M6's carried steps ([M6.md §8](M6.md#8-carried-into-m7)) have been run with the owner, and what they found is fixed or written down here.
3. On a fresh Cloudflare account, the README's "Deploy to Cloudflare" button deploys Atlas with nothing else done by hand, and the app opens. What a new account needs turned on (Workers Paid, Containers, R2, Workers AI) is in the README, and the workspace deploys or the README says exactly what to do.
4. A tagged release builds the bundle and the Mac app. On the deployment, Atlas settings → Account → Updates shows the new version and its notes the day it's published; Update installs it in one click, with no terminal and no GitHub; Roll back brings the previous version back, and the data is all still there. With Update on its own turned on, the next release goes in by itself in the night, and in the morning Atlas says what it installed, with Roll back on the card.
5. The vault and every stored key and token are encrypted by Atlas with the deployment's own key: an export of the agent's storage shows none of their values, and everything still works after a deploy. Account → Take a copy of everything downloads one archive that opens on a Mac and on Windows, with the log, records, pages, skills and Library's files, and no key, password or sign-in in it. Go back in time to an hour ago brings Atlas back as it was then, after a hold, and Undo puts it forward again. On a second, throwaway deployment, Delete everything (the phrase and a hold) leaves an empty Atlas that the setup code opens, and the page said what's still on Cloudflare.

### Stage 6: jobs Atlas owns

22. "Make a 60-second launch video for Atlas" starts a job: Atlas makes a plan with its checkpoints ("approve the script", "approve the cut"), shows it in Jobs, and starts on it. The toolbar shows the job; Agenda lists it.

26. A deploy mid-job, and a day's wait on a checkpoint, cost nothing while waiting and lose nothing.

### Stage 6b: the correction (2026-10-25)

Decided with the owner after the first real job (VISION §4.1, §8.3, §21.1, §21.2, §21.4, §23.1, §23.7). It replaces stage 6's lines 22 to 25, which described the first design.

C1. On Home the whole conversation fills the page, with the composer at the bottom; showing something opens the stage from it, and closing the stage goes back to the thread. Voice turns are in the same thread.
C2. A reply to an ordinary message starts within 2 seconds. Its turn sends the brain about 20k tokens at most (the common tools, the prompt and about 6k of recent conversation), and from the second message within a few minutes most of them come from the cache (`run.end` in the log says how many, and how many were cached).
C3. "Make a launch video for Atlas to post on X" gets a short reply in Atlas's own words, and a card for the job under the message. The card says what Atlas is doing right now only while something is, and updates in place.
C4. The owner never asks how it's going: Atlas says when it has something to show, when it's stuck, and when it's done, in the thread; only a question and the result notify.
C5. Picking the music: the question brings three tracks that play in the thread, with a button each. Tapping one answers it in the thread and in Inbox at once, and the job goes on with that track without asking again. Saying "faster, like this one" on the card instead goes straight to the job, which answers there.
C6. Atlas talks about the job in the first person ("I found three tracks"), never about "the job", "the step", "the helper" or "the checkpoint".
C7. A deploy mid-job loses nothing, and a day's wait on a question costs nothing: the job carries on from what it remembers, its notes and its folder.
C8. The finished video is a message in the thread with the file, and in Library; at Detailed level the card shows its notes, the coding agent's terminal and what it cost.
C9. A ChatGPT or Claude export moves in from setup or Library (line 36).
C10. The Jarvis bar's scenarios in `bench/` pass against the deployment (VISION §23.7).

The whole stage passes when the owner makes the launch video from asking to the file without opening Jobs or Inbox and without asking for status.

**Running the Jarvis bar (C10).** `ATLAS_OWNER_SECRET=... npm run eval:jarvis -- --url https://atlas.<you>.workers.dev` plays the scenarios in `bench/jarvis.json` over the app's socket: quick turns, a vague request, something Atlas can't do, a job that needs the owner's ears, "how's it going?" mid-job, a site behind a bot check, and an answer it mustn't ask for again. For each it records the time to the first text and to the run's end, the reply, the tools and the tokens in from `run.end`; makes the hard checks (first text under 2 s and about 20k tokens at most on a quick turn, no "the job", "the step", "helper", "checkpoint", "not pretending", "I won't pretend" or "that's on me", short replies without headers); and has a judge brain on the deployment (`POST /api/judge`, the character check's judges) read each conversation against what a person who's good at this would do. It prints a table and exits non-zero on any failure; the full results go to `bench/results/jarvis-<time>.json`. `--only hey,status` plays some, `--no-judge` skips the judge, and `--job-wait 120` shortens the wait on a job. The turns land in the owner's real thread; the bench stops every job it started and undoes the reminders it made, even after Ctrl-C.

Findings from the first Jarvis bar run (2026-10-25, 1 of 9 passed): each call was about 19k tokens and almost none came from the cache, even the second call within one turn. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 and later cache only at explicit breakpoints (`prompt_cache_breakpoint` on a message's content, `prompt_cache_options`), so Atlas now marks the last two of the owner's messages in a conversation's turns, and drops them if ChatGPT refuses them; each run's end records every call's cached tokens and whether the key and breakpoints were taken. A turn reads about 6k tokens of conversation now. The character makes an open request concrete with one plan, says plainly it can't hear audio and brings the thing to play, answers "how's it going?" with what's done on the thing meant, and hands long work over instead of researching while the owner waits; helpers are back among the common tools, and a job researches across many options with helpers in parallel. The eval now measures the first thing the owner sees (words, or the status line on a lookup) and tokens per call.

Second run (2026-10-25, 3 of 9): ChatGPT refused the explicit breakpoints, and with a one-word prompt gpt-5.6-terra starts answering in 1.4 to 1.8 s on its own (gpt-5.6-luna about 0.9 s, Claude Haiku 5.5 about 0.6 s, measured with Settings' live brain test), so the 2-second line needs a faster brain for main, which is the owner's choice. Found on the deployment: a job's own actions (a page read, the browser) showed as receipts in the main thread, and an answer typed on a question showed twice (on the question and as a bubble). A job's turns and its helpers now write their tool rows as the job's (`body.job`), which shows them in the job's thread only, and the owner's words and answers to a job stay on the question and in the job's thread. A job's turn's end isn't broadcast to the thread. The character answers "how's it going?" about the thing meant without the plumbing, and doesn't bring up work in progress unprompted.

Third run (2026-10-25, on `01e747d`, 4 of 9): first words or status in a median 1.7 s (p90 2.0 s), but a median 48k tokens in a turn (p90 89k) and 8% from the cache, against about 20k. What failed: "hey" was read as more of the launch video talk before it (100k tokens, five tools, a status report, and words passed to the video job); "help me plan the first day" became a job (`job_own`) instead of an answer; "how's it going?" right after asking for laptop research reported on the video, because the laptops went to a helper (`delegate`) that `jobs_look` doesn't show; "plan something fun for Saturday" left the venue and tickets for the owner to check; and the track picks came as a report with caveats, without asking for a pick. Most turns started with `tools_load` or `skill_read`, which is where the tokens go.

Fixed after the third run (2026-10-25): `job_own` says long work is making something or comparing many options over many minutes, and a plan, pick or answer that a search or two gives goes in the reply (its example had been "plan the Lisbon trip"), and the character says the same; a plan is checked (it's on, the time, tickets) before it's given; `skill_read` is a common tool, and the skills index says a chat, a question or a quick task needs no skill (most failing turns had loaded the skills group and read an unrelated skill, which pulled them toward the launch video); `jobs_look` lists the reading Atlas sent off from the conversation, newest first; `job_answer` passes only what the owner says about that work; and a helper's report is a few lines with links that asks for a pick when one is wanted. A helper's actions are now its card's (`body.job`), the main composer shows only the conversation's turns, and a job's composer shows its own. In a job's thread, each thing a turn does shows where it happened in its words as it finishes, with what it's doing now at the foot, and a finished turn shows its chips before its words. Library's overview had its files count fixed at 0; it lists the newest pages and files.

Fourth run (2026-10-25, on `0aaf930`, 4 of 9; an earlier run the same hour hit the code before the deploy had landed, which showed as helpers' rows without their job): first words or status in a median 1.6 s (p90 2.2 s), a median 34k tokens a turn (from 53k) and 34% from the cache; "hey" is one 15k-token call. "Plan something fun for Saturday", the audio question and the bot wall pass. Atlas's context now says the owner sees background work on its cards so it isn't brought up unasked, and that a status question is about that one thing alone; helpers are described by when they started and what they're reading, not a count of lookups; and a coding agent building for work Atlas owns isn't listed again as a coding job of its own, in the context or in `jobs_look` (the video's five Claude Code passes had been). Found on the deployment (2026-10-25, the launch video's approved cut): after the owner approved it, the job said "the workspace has reset again" and rebuilt the cut from scratch, though the cut was in Library; and when the owner said "stop the claude code", it said it couldn't. A job's folder (`/srv/jobs/<id>`, with its coding agent's `repo/` and renders) was outside `/workspace`, so nothing kept it: only the agent's git-tracked files were, as a bundle, and the container's disk goes when it sleeps after 10 minutes. ### Stage 6c: the wow pass (2026-10-26)

Decided with the owner after an audit of the deployment and the code on 2026-10-25 and 26, and a UI proposal with mockups ([docs/ui/source/wow-pass.html](ui/source/wow-pass.html)). The app met the locked design but read as stiff: technical text at the Simple level, answers as text in boxes, chrome before Atlas, and turns that are slow to start. The stage is built in phases, each closed by its lines here on the deployment.

**6c.1 Simple means simple.**
W1. At the Simple level the thread shows no model, provider, brain fallback, millisecond, raw kind or run id. "Answered by … because …" shows only in History and at Developer. A skill the review improved appears in History, not in the conversation or on Now.
W2. A message carries no "No area" chip and no "Voice" tag at Simple; its time shows on hover. Lookups (searches, page reads) fold into one quiet line per reply that opens to its sources; changes keep their receipt with Undo. Receipts are set in the interface's font, with mono only at Detailed and Developer.
W3. History lists each action once, with no raw kind column below Developer. A receipt opened at Simple says what Atlas did and offers Undo, without the tool's input or output.

Built 2026-10-26, not yet checked on the deployment: the thread's filter takes the detail level; a receipt from a tool of kind `look` that shows and changes nothing is marked `look`, and the thread folds those into one line ("Looked at yahoo.com") that opens to each; Atlas's own runs that said nothing (`surface: "none"`, no message) keep their receipts in History; History drops a web, map or weather delivery that repeats a receipt of the same run, and names brains as people know them.

**6c.2 What's shown is true.**
W4. A service that needs the owner (signed out, needs its key) is in Inbox and on Now, with the fix one tap away, wherever Abilities shows it.
W5. Library's overview never says "None yet" beside a count above 0.
W6. People opens on the people Atlas deals with, newest contact first; the rest of the address book is folded under one line. No-reply and bulk senders don't become people. Trust and area are blank when they aren't set.
W7. An automation is suggested only for a request asked on three separate days in a repeating shape, one at a time, calmly. The Jarvis bar's turns don't count toward suggestions, skill reviews or memory. Space starts voice only when nothing else has focus, and the composer never covers the end of a page.

Built 2026-10-26, not yet checked on the deployment: Inbox takes a `service` item from the extension desk for each service that's on and can't work (a lapsed sign-in, a missing key, an error newer than its last success), with Sign in or Add its key (opening its card) and Turn it off, and it leaves Inbox when fixed; Library's overview reads its places on their own; People leads with whom Atlas last dealt with (`touched_at`, one grouped query over sense, delivery and face rows) and folds the rest of the address book, with trust and area blank when unset; suggestions count distinct days in the owner's time zone, so a sitting of asks (the Jarvis bar's runs) is one, show one at a time, and ones counted by hours wait until asked again; Space starts voice only from the page itself. The composer already pads the page so it never covers its end. Found: Mapbox's card asks for two tokens in two boxes (the extension's and the catalog's setup), which reads as one too many.

**6c.3 Fast.**
W8. In voice, the brain that answers is the one measured fastest to start, a slow start isn't a failure, and Workers AI isn't waited on for 2.5 s first. Half of voice replies start speaking within a second of the owner finishing (VISION §11.2), measured in `voice.metrics`.
W9. A second message within a few minutes reads most of its prompt from the cache: the conversation is kept as a summary plus messages that only grow at the end, and what rarely changes (skills, files, services, areas) sits in the cached part. Tools loaded in a conversation stay loaded until the next summary.
W10. Nothing is awaited before the brain is called that the brain could be told instead (the nudge check, MCP servers still connecting). The owner's turn never waits behind one of Atlas's own runs.

Built 2026-10-26, not yet checked on the deployment: voice's chain goes in the order each brain was measured to start (`chain(…, { fastest })`, a brain not yet measured first once), and a brain beaten by the hedge is timed (`noteSlow`), not cooled, so it no longer drops to the back of the text chain; a turn's window of the conversation starts only at fixed points every half a budget from the summary (`windowStart`), so it stays put for several turns; skills, pages, brains and servers sit in the cached part of the owner's turns; groups of tools a conversation loaded stay loaded until its next summary (`turn_groups`), in the groups' order; a nudge's reaction is classified beside the turn, which gets a plain note at once; MCP servers still connecting are waited for by `mcp_tools` and `mcp_call` when used, not before every turn; the skill review runs beside the owner's turns. Still to see on the deployment: two ChatGPT calls seconds apart in one turn both read 0 cached tokens on 2026-10-25 (`cache_key: sent`, breakpoints refused), so the ChatGPT route may not cache at all; the Jarvis bar's run shows whether these changes are enough.

**6c.4 How Atlas sounds and learns.**
W11. Atlas doesn't announce a lookup before making it ("Let me check"), leads with the answer, and doesn't bring up background work unasked. "What does tomorrow look like?" gets a judgment and what matters, not a list of nothing.
W12. "Stop" in a job's thread stops its coding agent and helpers at once, and the job says so.
W13. The skill review reads only what happened since its last look, tool calls included, and runs only after a correction or a change, not after lookups. A news question changes no skill.

Built 2026-10-26, not yet checked on the deployment: the text profile says the app shows a lookup on its own, so Atlas looks without announcing it and its first words are the answer; the character leads a day or a week with what it's like and the one thing that matters, and doesn't bring up work it already handed over; a job told it can't finish while handed-off work runs is pointed at `job_stop_work` (built the day before, with the deploy still to come); the skill review gets a transcript of the owner's turns since its last look with each tool and what came back, without the conversation as history, and runs only when those turns changed something at least twice (lookups and `tools_load` don't count).

**6c.5 The shell and the front page.**
W14. Home has a focus layout: the sidebar folds to icons, the toolbar and frame step back, and Search and Talk stay one key away. The stage grows out of what was tapped as one sheet, with a spring, and the thread steps aside.
W15. The first open of the day shows the presence at full size: the living diamond, one useful line, the next thing with its route drawing itself and a countdown to leave, the weather to scale, and what needs you with hold to confirm. What finished while the owner was away shows as itself (a video's first frame that plays).
W16. Motion has one spring, numbers count to their value, a sent message lifts into place, loading shows the shape of the page with the diamond at rest, and undo plays the change backward. Every place but Home loads when it's first opened.

Built 2026-10-26, not yet checked on the deployment (screenshots from a local server with a sample morning: [front page](ui/screenshots/m7/wow-front-page.png), [on a phone](ui/screenshots/m7/wow-front-page-phone.png), [focus](ui/screenshots/m7/wow-focus.png)): on Home the sidebar folds to its icons (Inbox's count as an ember square), the toolbar loses its band, and the stage's rails, nodes and the thread's sheet fade; every other place keeps its frame. - **Stage 2:** whether the deploy button provisions Containers (its docs list Durable Objects, R2 and Workers AI, but not Containers); which permissions the Cloudflare token needs to upload a version and roll back; whether a Durable Object migration can ride in a version upload, or a release with one needs a full deploy through the API (and so can't be rolled back past).
  Checked on 2026-10-24, from Cloudflare's docs (to confirm on a fresh account):
  - **The button** copies the repo into the owner's GitHub as a new repo (not a fork), which needs to be public, and builds it with Workers Builds, which redeploys on every push to it. It provisions Durable Objects, R2 and Workers AI, but its docs don't mention Containers, Worker Loaders or Browser Run. Cloudflare's own containers template has a button, and Atlas's workspace uses the managed image chosen at run time, so no Docker build is needed. The account needs Workers Paid (Containers and Dynamic Workers) and R2 turned on.
  - **Monorepos aren't fully supported:** the button can point at a subdirectory only if it's self-contained, and `core/` reads `../app/dist` and `../extensions`. So the Wrangler config moves to the repo root (`main: core/src/index.ts`, assets at `app/dist`), with root `build` and `deploy` scripts the button reads.
  - **Secrets** are listed in `.dev.vars.example` and asked for at setup, with help text from `package.json`; nothing makes a random value.
  - **An update from the app:** an assets upload session, then a version upload with the bindings restated and the secrets inherited, then a deployment of that version at 100%. Rollback is a deployment of the previous version (with `force` when a secret changed since). The token needs Workers edit rights on this one Worker. The sizes are well inside the limits.
  - **Durable Object class changes** (a new class, a migration) can't ride in a version: such a release is a full script upload, and nothing rolls back past it. Each release says whether it's one, and the Updates page says so before updating. A version change resets each Durable Object, so a run cut off by an update must end as failed or deferred, with the reason.
  - **Workers Builds would overwrite an update** the next time the owner's copy is pushed. Setup disconnects it, or Atlas warns when a version built from the repo appears.
- **Stage 4:** Anthropic through AI Gateway with streaming, tool calls and cache control; how a plan's limit shows in ChatGPT's responses (its reset time), so a pause can say when.

  - **AI Gateway** (developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway: the Anthropic provider page, Authentication, and the Workers binding methods).   - **Skills carry over unchanged:** both use the Agent Skills SKILL.md format (frontmatter with `name` and `description`, then the body), which Atlas's skills already read, so a folder installs as a hub's does, its own SKILL.md kept as it came.

Atlas relies on its address's key, and each call's stream needs a one-time key from a call Atlas answered or placed. Checking the signature too would mean the owner pasting a second value in setup.
