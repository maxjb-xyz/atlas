Proposal · UX redesign · 2026-10-26

One Atlas, many threads

Atlas today is one endless conversation that does one thing at a time. This proposal splits the work into threads that run side by side, turns areas into spaces that feel like dedicated bots, and keeps the one mind and one memory that make Atlas worth using. Four nouns replace nine places.

Everything goes through one door, one at a time

These come from the code and from the feel audit run on the deployment this morning.

2 · What the market does

Two ways to do parallel work, and one of them is losing

"Grok bot" and "dots" are both from the last three months. Grok Bot (xAI with Cursor, beta since August 11) is a team of named bots. OpenAI's dots (DevDay, September 29) is one agent per person. Between them, Hermes and OpenClaw, every product picks one of two shapes:

  • Many bots, one chat each. Grok Bot, Hermes' Bot Mode and OpenClaw's multi-agent routing. Each bot has its own name, memory and endless conversation.
  • One agent, many threads. OpenAI's dots, Claude's Dispatch and Cursor's agents window. You keep talking to one agent, and background work shows up as titled threads with a state.

The first shape collects the loudest complaints: context mixes in the one long chat, every turn costs more, there's no way to start fresh, bots that wake each other burn the quota, and memory is split. Grok Bot's staff are now adding channels and a thread view to patch it. Atlas should take the second shape and give it what none of them have: a sidebar that shows all the work at once, and spaces with real data behind them.

3 · The model

Four nouns

Everyone already knows this shape from Slack, iMessage and Claude: a person you talk to, conversations with them, rooms for parts of your life, and a list of what's waiting on you. Atlas keeps one mind, one memory and one log underneath all four.

Atlas

The line to Atlas itself, always at the top. Quick asks, small talk, voice, and where Atlas texts you first. It stays short because work moves out of it.

Replaces: Home's thread

⠹Threads

One per piece of work or topic: "Launch video", "Lisbon trip", "Laptop under $1,500". Each has its own context and runs at the same time as the others. A job is a thread Atlas is working in.

Replaces: jobs, the jobs button, job cards

KSpaces

Your areas as rooms that feel like dedicated bots: Keyframes, School, Home, Money. Each has its own feed of what Atlas noticed and did there, its threads, its routines and its rules.

Replaces: areas, the scope switcher

Needs you

The top of the sidebar: every thread waiting on a decision, an approval or a look. Answer it there or in its thread; it's the same message.

Replaces: Inbox as a place

That's also rule 3 at work: threads are one mechanism that replaces three (jobs as their own concept, Inbox as a place, the scope switcher). Agenda and Library stay. Automations, People, Abilities and History move under one Atlas page beside settings, still one click and one question away.

4 · On the desktop

The sidebar is mission control

The sidebar stops being a list of pages and becomes a list of what's going on: what needs you, what Atlas is working on, your spaces, and recent threads. You can see five things running in parallel without opening any of them.

5 · Spaces: the bot feel without the bot problems

Each space feels like its own bot

The appeal of bots is real: a named thing that owns one part of your life, has its own feed, does its job without being asked, and that you can message directly. A space gives you all of that. It has a name, an icon and a color, its own feed, its own rules ("in Money, always ask before paying"), its own routines, and you can @mention it from anywhere.

What it doesn't have is its own memory or its own character. Separate bots can't answer "can I afford Lisbon after the Keyframes invoice?", because Money doesn't know Keyframes and neither knows your trip. Under every space is the same Atlas, so it can. This keeps VISION §8.4 (one relationship, many hands) and gives it a face people already understand.

6 · How it behaves

Rules that keep it simple

Where a message goes

  • What you type in a thread or space goes there. No guessing.
  • In the Atlas line, quick asks get answered right there. Long work (what job_own decides today) starts a thread, shown as a card you can open.
  • A message that clearly continues an open thread goes to it, with "Went to Lisbon trip · Undo". One tap moves it back.
  • @Keyframes draft the invoice from anywhere starts a thread in that space.
  • Any message has "Move to a thread", for when a chat turns into a project.

Parallel

  • Each thread has its own line of turns, so threads run at the same time. Within one thread, turns stay in order.
  • A new message in a busy thread stops its turn and keeps its work, as a job's thread already does.
  • A cap (proposed: four at once) keeps the brain bill and rate limits sane; past it, a thread waits and says so on its row.

One memory

  • Every thread writes to the one log and the same records, so a fact learned in one is known in all.
  • Every turn sees one line per open thread ("Launch video: waiting on the owner to pick a track"), so "how's the video going?" works from anywhere.
  • Each thread reads only its own recent messages and summary. That's why "hey" stops being read as video talk, and why turns get cheaper.

Staying tidy

  • Atlas names threads. Ones nobody touched in three days leave Recent and stay in search. Pin what you live in.
  • Only a question and a result notify, as jobs do today. Everything else updates the thread's row.
7 · On the phone and in Telegram

The same shape everywhere

On the phone the sidebar is the first screen, like Messages. In Telegram, each thread can be a topic in the chat with Atlas, which Telegram's bot API has supported in private chats since version 9.3 (one open report says sends to them broke in 10.0, so this gets checked first). Where topics aren't available, Atlas names the thread at the top of its message, and replying to a message routes to its thread.

8 · Why this beats Hermes and OpenClaw

They make you choose between focus and memory. Atlas doesn't.

Threads without forgettingIn Hermes and OpenClaw a fresh context means /new, which drops what the chat knew, and a separate bot means separate memory. In Atlas every thread starts clean and still knows everything Atlas knows.
All the work in one glanceTheir sub-agents live in a panel inside one chat. Atlas's sidebar shows every thread's state at once, across spaces, with what needs you on top.
Bots with something behind themA Hermes bot is a soul file and a memory file. An Atlas space has the area's people, calendar, mail account, tone, rules and routines, because those are already records.
Proactive without the billOpenClaw retired its heartbeat after users paid for idle checks. Atlas posts to a space's feed from its one intake, which wakes the main brain only for what matters.
Topics in Telegram by defaultOpenClaw binds threads to Telegram topics if you configure it. Atlas does it out of the box, with a fallback where topics aren't on.
An app, not a config fileSpaces, threads and their rules are made by asking or with a click. Nothing here needs a terminal (VISION rule 6).

Taken from the others, because they work: a state on every thread (Dispatch, Linear), your message overriding background work (Grok Bot), results folding into the main line as one collapsed line (Hermes), approvals waiting in the thread that raised them (OpenClaw), and progress to one channel with decisions to another (dots). Left behind: bots that talk to each other, a forever chat with no fresh start, and a heartbeat that resends everything.

9 · How it maps to the code

Most of it already exists, as jobs

A job is already "a conversation of its own" (VISION §8.3): its own transcript, its notes, a card, a thread one tap away, turns that stream, and words that interrupt a turn. A thread is a job that doesn't have to be working. So the build generalizes jobs rather than adding a mechanism.

10 · Smoke test, written before building

Done when these pass on the deployment

  1. Asking for laptop research and then "is it gonna rain?" gets the weather answer within 2 seconds while the research runs; the sidebar shows the research as Working.
  2. Three threads work at once (research, a coding job, a trip plan), each with its own row and state, and none waits on another.
  3. "hey" in the Atlas line, while a video thread is working, gets a hey back, one brain call, and nothing about the video.
  4. "How's the video going?" from the Atlas line, from another thread, and by voice each answers about the video.
  5. A fact told in one thread ("I'm vegetarian") is used in another the same hour.
  6. A message continuing the Lisbon plan, typed in the Atlas line, lands in Lisbon trip with Undo; Undo moves it back.
  7. A thread's question shows under Needs you on the desktop, the phone and Telegram, and answering it in any of them clears it in all.
  8. Keyframes' feed shows Atlas's nudge about the moved shoot; replying to it opens a thread in Keyframes, in its tone and from its email account.
  9. "@Money can I afford the Lisbon flights?" from the Keyframes space answers with what Atlas knows of both.
  10. In Telegram, the video's question arrives in the video's topic, and a reply there goes to that thread.
  11. A deploy while two threads are working loses nothing in either.
  12. A fifth thread started while four are working says it's waiting, and starts when one finishes.

Regression: M7's lines C1 to C10 and W1 to W23 still pass, read with "job" meaning a working thread.

11 · For you to decide

Five choices before building

Spaces that feel like bots, or separate bots?Spaces with one mind underneath. Separate bots split memory, which is what makes cross-area questions fail, and it's the shape Grok Bot's users complain about most.
Call them spaces, or keep "areas"?Spaces in the app; "area" stays the word in the code and VISION's data model, so nothing underneath renames.
Should Atlas move messages to threads on its own?Only for long work and for a clear continuation of an open thread, always with Undo. Everything else stays where it was typed.
How many threads at once?Four working at once, shown in Settings → Brains with what it means for cost.
Before launch or after?Before. It's the first thing a new user feels, and stages 1 and 2 are mostly reshaping jobs, which already work.
