Atlas · UI proposal · Oct 26, 2026

    
From a well-made tool to a presence.

    
Atlas is tidy, consistent and calm, and that's the problem: every answer is text in a box, every page is a list in a dashed frame, and the only thing alive is a small mark. Seven moves below, each with a live mockup in Atlas's own look. Press Replay on any of them. Names, places and numbers in the mockups are examples.

  






  

    
Why it reads as stiff

    
From the live deployment and the locked UI. None of these break the design language; they're about how it's used.

  

  

    
One weight
Body text at 16px, mono labels at 11px, hairline boxes around everything. Nothing is ever big, so nothing feels like a moment.

    
Answers are text
Atlas has hourly weather, routes, the calendar, videos and tracks, but shows them as sentences or a bulleted list in a card.

    
Chrome first
Sidebar of eight places, a toolbar, page tabs, dashed rails, a frame and a floating composer. Home looks like software before it looks like Atlas.

    
Logs as UI
Mono chips with raw search queries, "Answered by chatgpt:gpt-6-luna", "No area" on every message. The work is visible, but as a log.

    
Small motion
Chips tick in and a spinner turns. There's no orchestrated moment where the screen itself changes because Atlas did something.

    
No real things
No map, no faces, no site icons, no video frames, no cover art. VISION asks for real things; the screens are still grey and type.

  







  

    
01

    

      
Answers are things, not sentences

      
When the answer has a shape, draw the shape. "What does tomorrow look like?" should give one line of judgment and a day you can read in a second: time to scale, the walk between things, the weather over it, the free gaps, and "leave by" in ember. This is generative UI built on the typed views Atlas already has (`show`), drawn inline in the thread instead of as a list card.

02

    

      
The grid comes alive when Atlas works

      
The dotted stage grid is Atlas's medium; let it be the thing that moves. While Atlas thinks, the dots near its mark turn into characters from the diamond's ramp and ripple out, like the mark is radiating into the page. Each tool lights a node on a short rail, one status line swaps verbs, and the answer arrives word by word with sources as real site marks. Still characters and opacity only: no glow, no orb.

What changes
    
          
  • One status line with a verb that swaps in place ("Searching", "Reading three pages", "Checking today") replaces a stack of mono chips.
    • 
          
  • Sources as site marks with how fresh they are, folded into one row. Raw queries go to Detailed.

    • 
          
  • The grid ripple is a canvas behind the thread, capped at 30 fps, off with reduced motion.
    • 
          
  • VISION change: §21.8 limits the diamond to Now, the day's presence and voice. This extends its characters into the grid while Atlas works.
    • 
        

        
Borrowed from
    
          
  • Claude Code's spinner: one line, a verb that changes, a timer. Only one glyph moves, so it stays calm.
    • 
          
  • Linear's agent sessions: one explicit state and the latest thought, updated in place, never a log.
    • 
          
  • Streamdown: each word fades in over about 150 ms with a 4 px rise.
    • 
          
  • Codrops' shape-aware ASCII renderer (Sep 2026): characters as a live medium, not a font.

03

    

      
Voice is the demo, so make it the hero

      
This is the moment people film. In the conversation, voice shows live captions and the cards it looks up, which is where ChatGPT moved its voice mode in late 2025. Then give it a full-screen mode for hands-free use: the diamond large and reacting to the real audio, your words faint above, Atlas's words typeset huge and lighting up as they're spoken, and the things it looked up assembling underneath. Pair it with the latency fixes from the audit: none of this lands at 3.5 seconds to first word.

04

    

      
Home opens like a front page

      
The first screen of the day is the strongest sell Atlas has, and today it's a small strip in the thread. Make the presence big: the living diamond, one line that's actually useful, then three real things. A map whose route draws itself with a countdown to leave, the weather to scale, and what needs you with hold to confirm. Then what happened while you were away, as the thing itself: the video's first frame, ready to play.

05

    

      
Less chrome around the conversation

      
On Home, the frame, rails, toolbar and page tabs all say "app" before Atlas says anything. Give Home a focus layout: the sidebar folds to an icon rail, the toolbar and frame fade, and the conversation sits on the open grid. When Atlas shows something, the stage slides in as one full-height sheet and the thread steps aside, sharing the element you tapped. The frame and rails stay where they mean something: the stage.

06

    

      
One command bar that knows everything

      
⌘K is where a power user decides whether a product is serious, and today "launch" finds nothing. Make it search everything Atlas has (files with their frames, jobs with live state, skills, people with faces, moments in the conversation) and offer actions in words. Try typing below: "launch", "french", "alvarez" or "quiet".

07

    

      
Small things that signal craft

      
None of these is a feature. Together they're why an app feels expensive. Each one is cheap and stays inside the motion budget (transforms and opacity).

    

  

  

    

    

      
Springs, not easings
Sheets, cards and selections settle with a little overshoot. One spring curve for the whole app.

      
Numbers count to their value
Counts and costs roll up on arrival with tabular figures so nothing jitters. Times and temperatures can decode instead: characters cycle briefly, then lock in, which suits the ASCII mark.
$0.00

      
A sent message lifts away
Your words rise from the composer into the thread, and a sent email lifts off its card. VISION lists it; it isn't built.
Wednesday works, see you at 2.

      
Sound and haptics, quiet
A soft tick when a receipt lands, a low note when something needs you, a click on hold to confirm. Off by default on desktop, on with haptics on the phone.

      
Loading is never a sentence
"Connecting to Atlas." becomes the diamond at rest and the thread's shape in skeleton, so the page has its layout from the first frame.

      
Undo rewinds
Undo plays the change backward (the event slides back to Wednesday) instead of the item just reappearing.

      
Real things in every list
Site icons on sources, photos and area-colored initials on people, service logos, video frames, map tiles. Grey squares and file glyphs only when there's truly nothing.

      
Type with a scale
One display size for moments (46px), one for answers that are numbers (30px), body at 16px, mono only for times and tags. Today almost everything is 16px or 11px mono.

Order of work

      
Start with what everyone sees in the first minute, and fix the audit's speed and plumbing items alongside, since motion can't hide a 4-second wait or a model id in the thread.

    

  

  

    

    

      

        
Week 1
Focus layout on Home (05), the front page as Home's empty state (04), springs and count-ups everywhere (07).
Mostly `app/src/Shell.tsx`, `pages/Home.tsx` and `styles.css`. No core changes.

        
Week 2
Status line, sources with site marks, words rising in, then the living grid (02).
Receipts carry a host and a favicon; the thread groups lookups per run. One VISION change for the grid.

        
Week 3
Inline views: day, route, weather, compare, media (01), with follow-up chips.
Views exist as typed contracts; this adds compact inline renderers and a line in the character about leading with judgment.

        
Week 4
Full-screen voice with timed captions (03), after the voice latency fix. Then ⌘K over everything (06).
Word timings from ElevenLabs; a search route over records, files, jobs, skills and the log.
